# Vertical Command — National Self-Serve OS for One SMB Vertical

**One-liner** — Take everything Strelva already does for a local business — answer-native site, AI-visibility scoring, governed content agent, weekly receipt — collapse it into one self-serve product purpose-built for a *single national vertical* (lead candidate: **med-spas / aesthetics clinics**), and sell it as the category's operating system at $199–$499/month with no Jacob in the loop. Vertical focus replaces geographic focus: instead of owning Buffalo, own every med-spa in America.

---

## The bet (high-MRR thesis) — why this can grow FAST and BIG, not slow and local.

The prior synthesis (Combination A) optimized the two scarce inputs of a 2-person Buffalo studio — founder hours and trust — into a slow, compounding *local* monopoly. That bet is correct for safety and wrong for velocity. It caps at Jacob's build capacity and one city's trade population. The research digest is blunt about why: **services trade at 1x–3x revenue, SaaS at 3x–10x+; every $400M+ ARR case is horizontal-or-vertical-national, never one geography; vertical SMB SaaS in a *narrow* niche caps (~$29k MRR funeral-home story) but a *national* vertical with deep workflow lock-in does not.** The funeral-home ceiling is a TAM problem, not a vertical-SaaS problem. Pick a vertical big enough and the ceiling moves to nine figures.

The high-MRR wager has four legs:

1. **Vertical depth produces ACV and lock-in that horizontal builders (Wix, Squarespace, Framer, GoDaddy Airo) structurally cannot match.** A med-spa does not want a website builder; it wants the thing that books Botox consults, proves the new injector is filling her chair, ranks the clinic when ChatGPT is asked "best lip filler near me," and drafts the monthly membership-promo email — governed so nothing wrong about a *medical* service ever ships. That is a $300/month product to a business doing $40k–$120k/month in revenue. Horizontal tools sell a $16/month canvas to everyone and a workflow to no one. ACV is the entire game: at $300 ACV you need ~33 customers for $10k MRR; at $16 you need 625.

2. **One vertical = one schema, one set of integrations, one content model, one compliance posture — which is precisely what makes self-serve onboarding automatable.** Strelva's current blocker is that every site is bespoke. Bespoke is unavoidable *across* verticals (a law firm and a taco truck share nothing) but *evaporates within* one. Ten med-spas need the same six page types, the same booking integrations (Boulevard, Vagaro, Mangomint), the same JSON-LD (`MedicalBusiness`/`HealthAndBeautyBusiness`), the same AEO query set ("best [treatment] in [city]"). Productizing one vertical is a finite, shippable engineering problem; productizing "all local business" is not. **The vertical is the unlock that makes zero-touch onboarding possible at all.**

3. **AI-visibility is a fresh, urgent, vertical-shaped wound — and the existing audit engine is 70% of the wedge.** AI Overviews now cover ~48% of queries; AI-search traffic converts at 14.2% vs 2.8% organic; the AEO-monitoring category just printed Peec AI ($4M→$10M ARR in 6 months, >$100M valuation) and Profound ($96M raised, $1B valuation at ~$6.8M ARR). Every one of those players sells to enterprise marketing teams. **No one sells a $49–$99/month "does ChatGPT recommend your clinic?" product to a med-spa owner.** That gap is the cold-open. A free, vertical-specific audit ("Med-Spa AI Visibility Scorecard") is the PLG on-ramp the digest calls the highest-leverage existing asset.

4. **A vertical is a self-contained viral graph.** Med-spa owners are in the same Facebook groups, the same conferences (AmSpa, The Aesthetic Show), follow the same consultants, and benchmark obsessively against each other. A shareable scorecard with a percentile ("you rank in the bottom third of med-spas for AI visibility") detonates inside that graph in a way a generic "local business" tool never could, because the comparison is *to peers they care about*. National reach, peer-dense distribution, zero cold outbound.

The honest size: US med-spa market is ~10,000+ locations growing double-digits; aesthetics is one of the fastest-growing SMB segments in healthcare. Even modest penetration at $300 ACV clears eight figures of ARR. And the playbook is *clonable to a second vertical* (dental, vet, fitness studios, law) once the first is a machine — that is the path past $1M MRR.

## MRR mechanics — pricing model, ACV, who pays, expansion revenue, and a realistic ramp.

**Who pays:** the clinic owner / practice manager — not a marketing team, not an agency. One buyer, one card, self-serve checkout. This is the SMB-direct motion, and the vertical focus is what makes a $300 price land where a horizontal $300 price would not: the product speaks the buyer's exact language and removes a named pain.

**Pricing model — subscription floor + usage expansion (the hybrid the digest validates: usage-based delivers ~10% higher NRR, 22% lower churn, and NRR ≥100% grows 2x faster YoY):**

- **Free / Scorecard** — public AI-visibility audit + one-page benchmark vs. peer clinics. No login required to see the score; email gate to see the *fixes* and the percentile detail. Pure acquisition; $0. This is the wedge, not a tier to monetize.
- **Visibility — $99/month** — continuous AI-visibility monitoring (tracked weekly across ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Mode for a vertical-tuned query set), the answer-native site, schema kept correct, the weekly forwardable receipt. Entry product; lands the card.
- **Command — $299/month** — everything above + the governed AI content agent (auto-approve factual / review copy / block structural — the rare asset that matters *more* in a medical vertical), blog/promo drafting, review-response drafting, social drafts, the live "what AI says about you today" surface.
- **Command+ — $499/month** — adds the booking-conversion layer and multi-location (one dashboard, per-location receipts) — the natural expansion for the clinic chains that are the highest-LTV accounts.
- **Usage expansion** that lifts NRR above 100% without a new sale: per-extra-location, per-extra-tracked-query-set, per-blast of AI-drafted content beyond the monthly allotment, optional AI voice/booking-recovery at a per-conversation rate. Heavy clinics self-expand; that is the compounding NRR the digest names as the separator of fast trajectories.

**Blended ACV target:** ~$3,000–$3,600/year ($250–$300/month blended after mix and expansion). This is the number that makes the ramp tractable.

**Realistic ramp:**

- **$10k MRR (~35–70 paying clinics).** Requires: the free scorecard live and genuinely shareable; **zero-touch onboarding for this one vertical** (owner signs up, connects booking + Google profile, site is live from a vertical template same-day, no Jacob); Stripe self-serve billing on; the weekly receipt auto-generating. Reachable in ~3–6 months *if* the onboarding is automated — the digest's AEO-SMB comparable (Otterly, Visiblie, Peec) shows a $49–$199 monitoring product can hit this band fast with PLG behind it. This milestone is gated almost entirely on *automation shipping*, not on demand.
- **$100k MRR (~330–500 clinics).** Requires the growth loop turning on its own: scorecard shares → signups → receipts forwarded → peer signups, plus programmatic SEO/GEO pages ranking, plus a thin **white-label channel** (aesthetics consultants/agencies who manage 10–40 clinics each — the Vendasta multiplier: one partner = dozens of sub-tenants). This is the milestone where the *channel* matters more than direct PLG, and where NRR ≥100% from multi-location expansion does real work. 12–18 months on a working loop.
- **$1M MRR.** Requires either (a) saturating the first vertical hard *and* opening a second vertical on the same self-serve rails (the clone — dental or vet inherits ~80% of the platform), or (b) the white-label/agency channel becoming the dominant volume engine. The platform must be genuinely multi-vertical by now (vertical config, not vertical code). This is the "vertical SaaS factory" endgame — pick the vertical, generate the audit/schema/query-set/template config, ship a new national product in weeks. 24–36 months.

The honest read against benchmarks: only ~3.3% of SaaS hits $1M ARR in <12 months, but AI-native startups are 8x more likely to reach $10M ARR in 12 months. Vertical Command is AI-native (audit + governed agent are the product, not a bolt-on). $10k MRR fast is realistic; $1M MRR is a 2–3 year machine, not a quarter.

## Growth loop — the organic/PLG/viral engine (each user brings the next); time-to-traction.

**The loop, end to end:**

1. **Free vertical scorecard is the ad.** "See how AI describes your med-spa." Owner pastes their URL, gets a letter grade + a peer percentile + the specific gaps ("ChatGPT doesn't know you offer microneedling; 3 competitors are cited where you aren't"). This is the HubSpot Website Grader pattern (4M sites graded, 40k organic backlinks) aimed at one vertical so the score is *comparative to peers*, which is what makes it sting and share.
2. **Output is inherently shareable inside a peer-dense graph.** A med-spa owner who scores a B+ posts it; one who scores a D wants the fix. Every scorecard carries a "Powered by Strelva — score your clinic" footer (ClickFunnels attributed ~$1M+/month MRR to a badge alone). The share lands in front of *another med-spa owner*, not a random stranger — the vertical makes the impression qualified.
3. **The weekly receipt is the second viral surface.** Branded, one-page, plain-English, forwardable. Owners forward it to business partners and to their injectors as proof the marketing works. Footer: "get this for your clinic." Every receipt is an impression on a peer's inbox.
4. **Programmatic GEO/AEO pages compound nationally.** Auto-generate a public "[Clinic Name] AI Visibility Report" page for every indexed med-spa in the US (pulled from Google/Yelp data) — the Zapier programmatic-SEO model. National long-tail capture for "[city] med-spa AI visibility / website review," and statistics-dense benchmark content ("the average US med-spa scores C- on AI visibility") that ChatGPT/Perplexity cite in 2026 where 60%+ of searches have an AI component. Strelva becomes the *cited authority on med-spa web presence*, which feeds the top of the funnel for free.
5. **White-label/agency layer multiplies the same loop.** Aesthetics consultants run the scorecard on their book of clients as a prospecting tool; they bring 10–40 clinics onto the platform under their own brand. One agency signup = dozens of sub-tenants. This is bottom-up smuggling into the channel that the repo already calls a future option.

**Time-to-traction:** scorecard lead-gen starts immediately (day one). The viral peer-share loop turns on the moment the percentile is honest (needs a few dozen scored clinics to compute). Programmatic SEO ranks in 3–6 months and compounds for years. No cold outbound at any stage — the product is the distribution.

**The single number that proves the loop:** scorecard → free→paid conversion rate, and receipt forward-rate. If a scored owner converts at even half the digest's AEO-SMB freemium signal, and one in five receipts gets forwarded, the loop is self-propelling.

## Why it scales (software economics) — where marginal cost approaches zero.

The whole point of the vertical-national shape is to move the cost basis from services (30–50% gross margin, founder-hour-bound) to software (70–80%+, near-zero marginal cost per tenant). The four automations the digest names as the margin-flip prerequisites map directly:

1. **Zero-touch onboarding.** Owner signs up → picks from 3–4 vertical-tuned templates → connects Google Business Profile + booking platform via OAuth → site goes live from the vertical content model, pre-seeded with `MedicalBusiness` schema and an AEO-ready FAQ block. **No repo built by hand. No Jacob.** This is the load-bearing automation and the hardest engineering lift — but it is *finite* because it's one vertical, one schema, one integration set.
2. **Self-serve content activation without a custom repo.** Strelva already separates control plane from delivery via the versioned `/api/v1` contract + signed HMAC revalidation. The unlock is one **multi-tenant rendered storefront** (a single Next app serving every clinic from `/api/v1` by tenant) instead of N hand-built repos. Marginal cost of clinic N+1 becomes a database row, not a build.
3. **Automated billing + metering.** Stripe self-serve checkout, usage metering on tracked-query-sets / locations / content blasts. Already partially scaffolded (`STRIPE_SCAFFOLD_PRICE_ID`, `isBillingEnabled()`); needs turning on and metering wired.
4. **In-product first-value < 5 minutes.** The audit *is* the first value and it's instant. The governed agent making a real, visible change in the first session is the wow moment. The receipt landing in week one is the retention anchor (TTFV <7 days cuts churn ~50%).

Where marginal cost approaches zero: every tracked clinic after onboarding is a Redis/Sanity row + scheduled cron work (already how `weekly-brief.ts` and the poll-* crons operate). AI-visibility checks are API calls (ChatGPT/Perplexity) amortized across tenants by query-set caching. The governed agent runs on `gemini-2.5-flash` (cheap). The model cost stays well under 20% of revenue at $99–$499 ACV.

What is explicitly *not* founder-hour-bound anymore: site creation, content updates, monitoring, reporting, billing. What remains human: vertical *config* authoring (once per vertical, not per clinic), support, and trust/QA spot-checks. That is the services-to-software flip.

Already polling Yelp/Google reviews/Instagram on schedule.

## The pivot required — HONEST: what about today's model must change or be abandoned.

This is a real pivot, not a re-skin. Naming the gap is the job:

1. **Abandon the hand-built per-client repo as the delivery model for new customers.** The free custom repo is the binding constraint on MRR velocity — it makes clinic N+1 cost Jacob-hours. It must be replaced by a **single multi-tenant rendered storefront** that serves every clinic from `/api/v1`. This is the largest engineering lift in the whole approach and the prerequisite for everything else. The existing Rohlax/GLDF custom repos keep running on the back-compat layer; new customers never get a repo.

2. **Abandon "free site as the wedge"; adopt "free audit as the wedge."** Today the free hand-built site is the acquisition gift. That doesn't scale (Jacob-hours) and trains customers to expect bespoke. The free *tool* (scorecard) replaces it: zero marginal cost, shareable, national. The site becomes part of the *paid* product, not a free giveaway. This is the digest's explicit "free-site → free-tool" flip.

3. **Abandon "any local business"; commit to ONE vertical, nationally.** The horizontal templates (wellness, food-brand, restaurant, trades, professional, fashion-stylist) become legacy. The product, schema, query sets, templates, integrations, and marketing all narrow to one vertical. Breadth is the enemy of self-serve automation.

4. **Abandon the Buffalo geographic frame.** Buffalo trust is non-portable and irrelevant to a med-spa in Phoenix. The brand becomes a national vertical-SaaS brand ("the operating system for med-spas"), not a local Buffalo studio. The two-division Buffalo identity in the current synthesis is incompatible with this and must go.

5. **Demote bespoke to a concierge tier, not the engine.** Hand-built sites can survive as a premium "done-for-you" option (high price, low volume, Jacob-built), but they cannot be the growth motion. Self-serve is the engine; concierge is a tail.

What is *kept*: the governed agent, the audit engine, the `/api/v1` contract, the receipt, the data spine, the AI-visibility wedge. The pivot is in the *delivery model and the market focus*, not in the core technology — which is exactly why it's feasible.

**If the founder will not abandon hand-built free sites and the Buffalo local frame, this approach is dead and Combination A (the local trades monopoly) is the right answer.** There is no high-MRR version that keeps the agency delivery model. Say so plainly.

## Moat — what compounds and resists copying at scale.

- **Within-vertical benchmark data, at national scale.** "You rank in the bottom 30% of US med-spas for AI visibility" is a sentence only the company running thousands of med-spas on one schema can write. Horizontal builders (Wix, Framer) are vertical-agnostic by economic design — narrowing would shrink their TAM, so they won't, and they have no same-vertical tenant population to benchmark against. AEO-monitoring players (Peec, Profound) are vertical-agnostic enterprise tools — they have the monitoring but not the *vertical* benchmark or the *fix* layer. The benchmark compounds with every clinic and is the thing that makes the receipt un-cancellable.

- **The detect-and-fix flywheel no monitoring tool can replicate.** Peec/Profound tell a brand it's invisible in AI search; they cannot fix it. Strelva detects the gap *and* the governed agent closes it without the owner doing anything. Monitoring + the site + the governed agent in one loop is a moat no pure-monitoring tool and no pure-builder can assemble, because each owns only one half.

- **Governance as the medical-vertical license to operate.** In a vertical where wrong copy about a procedure is a liability, "an AI manages it, but factual changes auto-approve, copy gets reviewed, structural changes are blocked" is the differentiator a generic AI builder structurally cannot offer without rebuilding the governance engine.

- **Vertical workflow lock-in + switching cost.** Once a clinic's booking, reviews, content, schema, and reporting all run through Strelva, leaving means rebuilding the whole presence. Deep vertical integration is stickier than any horizontal canvas.

- **The clone advantage.** Once the first vertical is a self-serve machine, vertical #2 inherits ~80% of the platform — only config (audit factors, schema type, query set, templates) changes. The moat becomes the *factory*, not any single vertical.

## Risks & kill signal.

- **The onboarding automation is harder than scoped and Jacob stays in the loop.** If clinic N+1 still needs founder-hours after the multi-tenant storefront ships, the margin flip never happens and it's still an agency. **Kill signal:** 90 days after "self-serve" launch, median human-minutes-to-activate-a-clinic isn't trending toward zero.
- **Scorecard doesn't share / convert.** The whole loop assumes the peer-dense vertical graph forwards the score and the percentile stings enough to convert. **Kill signal:** after a few hundred scorecards run, free→paid conversion is far below the AEO-SMB comparables and receipt forward-rate is near zero. The PLG thesis is then wrong for this vertical.
- **AEO commoditizes before Strelva owns the SMB tier.** Peec/Profound or Semrush ship a cheap SMB-vertical product first. **Kill signal:** a credible $49 med-spa AI-visibility tool with the fix layer appears before Strelva has density. Mitigant: the *fix + governed agent* half is the hard part; pure monitoring is the easy half to copy and the weak half to own.
- **Wrong vertical (TAM too small or WTP too low).** If med-spas won't pay $299 or the market is thinner than it looks. **Kill signal:** blended ACV repeatedly lands below ~$150 across the first 30 paying clinics. Mitigant: med-spa unit economics ($40k–$120k/month revenue, double-digit growth, ad-spend-heavy) strongly suggest WTP is there; if not, the platform is built to re-point at a higher-WTP vertical (med/legal/dental).
- **Founder attention split.** Building self-serve infrastructure competes with billable hand-built work for the same two people. **Kill signal:** the platform stalls for a quarter because client work ate the roadmap. This is the classic services-trap failure and must be guarded with a hard time-boxed commitment to the platform.
