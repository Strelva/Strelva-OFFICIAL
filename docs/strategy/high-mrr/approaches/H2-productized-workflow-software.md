# Workflow Catalog OS — Self-Serve, Governed SMB Automations Sold Nationally By Subscription

**One-liner** — Take the four most common SMB back-office workflows (web-lead → CRM, estimate → invoice follow-up, booking → reminder/no-show recovery, new-client onboarding), turn each into a *named, fixed-scope, self-serve software product* a business owner activates in under ten minutes with no Jacob build step, priced as a per-workflow subscription, where the governed AI agent does the delivery and the owner just approves — sold to any SMB in the country off a public catalog, with a free "what's leaking out of your business" audit as the PLG front door.

---

## The bet (high-MRR thesis) — why this can grow FAST and BIG, not slow and local.

The prior Buffalo synthesis (Combination A) is correct *and structurally capped*: it amortizes Jacob's build cost across one dense local vertical, which is the right way to run a 2-person studio safely but is a services business wearing a SaaS costume. Each new logo still requires a human to configure, and the ceiling is Erie County's ~1,417 trades establishments. The honest read in the research digest is the same one: "funeral-home SaaS at $29k MRR in 12 months is the *ceiling* story, not the floor." A vertical SMB tool in one city is a five-figure-MRR business, full stop.

The bet here is that the **same four workflows are 80% identical across every 1–20-person service business in the United States, not just Buffalo trades** — a plumber in Phoenix, a dentist in Tampa, a med-spa in Austin, and a wedding photographer in Denver all lose web leads that never reach a CRM and all let estimates die without follow-up. The 20% that differs is *config* (which CRM, which booking tool, which fields, which copy), not engineering. If that holds, the catalog stops being "Jacob builds it for a Buffalo client" and becomes "any owner in the country self-serves a workflow product in ten minutes." That is the difference between a $30k-MRR local services business and the $3.2B (2026) → $17–31B (2033–35) AI-for-SMB market the digest sizes.

Why it can grow *fast*: the wedge is a free instant-value tool (the audit), the activation is self-serve (no human in the loop), and the output is a *recurring receipt the owner forwards* — which is both the retention anchor and the referral trigger. That is the exact shape of every fast-grower in the digest: Calendly (zero sales staff at launch), Loom ($975M acquisition, near-zero marketing, "every shared video is a Loom ad"), Lovable ($0→$17M ARR in 3 months, no paid ads). The product *is* the distribution engine.

Why it can grow *big*: horizontal-across-verticals TAM (every SMB with a website and a booking tool), software economics (the catalog item is built once and stamped by config N times), and expansion revenue baked in (one workflow → four workflows per account). The digest is unambiguous that *broad TAM + PLG wedge + usage/subscription hybrid* is the only shape that reaches $100k → $1M MRR, and that "every $400M+ ARR case is horizontal." This frame keeps Strelva's rarest asset — the production-proven governance engine — as the *differentiator inside a horizontal product*, which is exactly where the digest says differentiation must live: "the governed AI agent layer and the proof/dashboard layer — those are not yet commoditized."

## MRR mechanics — pricing model, ACV, who pays, expansion revenue, and a realistic ramp.

**Pricing model — subscription floor + per-workflow expansion + usage ceiling.** Three tiers, all self-serve, all national:

- **Free** — the audit + a read-only "here's what's leaking" report. No card. This is the wedge, not a product; it exists to manufacture problem-awareness and capture email.
- **One workflow — $49/month.** A single named automation, live, governed, with the weekly receipt. This is the Otterly-Lite-equivalent entry point ($29/mo in the AEO comps); we anchor slightly higher because the workflow *recovers revenue*, not just reports.
- **Workflow bundle (Business OS) — $149/month.** All four workflows + the dashboard + the AI content agent + the weekly receipt. This is the Lovable-Pro-tier anchor ($25/mo for a builder toy; $149/mo for a tool that recovers lost jobs is defensible because the ROI is one saved booking).
- **Usage ceiling on the high end** — heavy accounts (lead volume above a threshold, or multi-location) meter into an overage or a $299–$499 "multi-location / agency-managed" tier. The digest is explicit that usage/subscription hybrids deliver *10% higher NRR and 22% lower churn* than flat-rate, and that NRR ≥100% is the compound that makes companies grow 2x faster YoY. The usage ceiling is what pushes NRR above 100% without re-selling.

**Who pays:** the owner-operator of a 1–20-person service business, on their own card, self-serve — *not* a procurement committee, *not* via a Jacob sales call. ACV at the $149 bundle is ~$1,800/yr; at the $49 entry it's ~$590/yr. These are credit-card-impulse price points, which is what makes self-serve PLG possible — nobody schedules a demo to buy a $49/mo tool, they just turn it on.

**Expansion revenue (the real engine):** land on one workflow, expand to four. The receipt does the expanding for free — it surfaces the next leak in the owner's own data ("12 booking clicks last week, 4 became no-shows, none recovered — turn on No-Show Recovery"). Expansion is *in-product and zero-touch*, which is the only kind that scales. A second workflow on an existing account is pure margin: the build cost was already paid by client #1 of that workflow nationally.

**Realistic ramp:**

- **$10k MRR** ≈ ~67 bundle accounts, or ~200 entry accounts, or a blend. Requires: (a) the audit live as a public no-signup tool, (b) *one* workflow fully self-serve end-to-end (the highest-leverage one — web-lead → CRM), (c) Stripe self-serve checkout wired (currently `STRIPE_SCAFFOLD_PRICE_ID` is intentionally unpinned — that must change), (d) the audit→email→activate funnel instrumented. This is the "$10k MRR in 3–6 months with distribution behind it" claim in the digest, and it is reachable *only* if onboarding is zero-touch. It is **not** reachable on the hand-build model.
- **$100k MRR** ≈ ~670 bundle accounts. Requires: all four workflows self-serve, NRR ≥100% (expansion + usage offsetting churn), the programmatic-SEO surface live (a public "[Business Name] Business Health Report" page per indexed SMB, the Zapier model), and the white-label/agency channel opened as a 10–100x multiplier on reach. This is where the digest's agency lever (Vendasta: 66,000 partners, 8.2M SMBs, $92.9M ARR) becomes the difference between linear and compounding.
- **$1M MRR** ≈ ~6,700 bundle-equivalents, or a smaller count of agency/multi-location accounts at $299–$499. Requires: the catalog past four (each new workflow is a new expansion SKU and a new SEO surface), the AEO/AI-visibility monitor folded in as a fifth recurring SKU (the digest's highest-urgency wedge — Peec $4M→$10M ARR in 6 months, an unserved SMB tier), and the agency channel carrying the bulk of net-new logos. At this scale the founder-hour-per-account number must be ~zero or the model has failed.

## Growth loop — the organic/PLG/viral engine (each user brings the next); time-to-traction.

Three compounding loops, no cold outbound, in the exact shapes the digest validates:

1. **Free-audit wedge (the HubSpot Website Grader loop).** The any-URL audit is public, no-signup, instant: paste a URL, get an A–F grade and a list of *named leaks* ("you got 14 web leads last month — here's where they go to die"). The score is a psychological hook (shame/pride); the report is shareable; the email gate fires *after* proof. HubSpot's Grader graded 4M sites and generated 40,000+ organic backlinks. Strelva's audit engine (`/api/audit/scan`) already produces score + grade + categories + per-IP rate-limiting + caching — this is ~70% built. Time-to-traction: lead-gen *immediately*; SEO compounding over 6–18 months.

2. **Forwardable receipt (the Loom/Calendly multi-party loop).** Every workflow emits a weekly plain-English receipt — "we caught 9 web leads, recovered 2 no-shows, chased 3 stale estimates." The owner forwards it to a partner, an accountant, or *another owner*. The footer carries a "powered by Strelva — audit your own business free" badge. ClickFunnels attributed $1M+/month MRR (~20% of total) to a badge alone. Every receipt sent is a top-of-funnel impression at zero marginal cost.

3. **Programmatic product-led SEO (the Zapier loop).** Auto-generate a public "[Business Name] Business Health Report" page for every indexed SMB nationally, pulling Google/Yelp data the platform already touches. National long-tail capture ("[city] [trade] website audit", "is [business] losing leads"), compounding with GEO/AEO citation in ChatGPT/Perplexity because the pages are statistics-dense and structured. Pages start ranking in 3–6 months; the surface is millions of indexed pages from product data.

The loops feed each other: SEO pages and forwarded receipts both dump strangers into the free audit; the audit converts them to a $49 workflow; the workflow emits receipts and an SEO page; repeat. No sales team. The bottleneck — per the digest — is never the channel, it's whether the product has a "wow moment users share." The wow here is concrete: *the owner watches a real lost lead get caught in real time.*

## Why it scales (software economics) — where marginal cost approaches zero; what is automated.

The four automations the digest names as the prerequisite to flip from services margins (30–50%) to software margins (70–80%+) map directly onto what must be built:

1. **Zero-touch tenant onboarding.** Today provisioning is `pnpm provision-tenant` + Jacob building a repo. This must become a self-serve signup that creates the tenant, connects the owner's tools via OAuth, and activates a workflow — no human. Strelva already has multi-tenant host resolution, Sanity/Redis tenant config, and per-tenant data stores; the missing piece is the *self-serve front door*, not the back end.
2. **Self-serve workflow activation without a custom repo.** The whole point. A workflow is a hardened template + a thin config the owner fills via a guided setup, not a repo Jacob writes. The governed AI agent executes the workflow's decision points; the owner approves the ambiguous ones. Marginal engineering cost of account N of a given workflow ≈ the OAuth call and a config row.
3. **Automated billing + metering.** Stripe self-serve checkout, per-workflow line items, usage metering for the ceiling tier. The plumbing exists (`src/lib/subscription.ts`, `isBillingEnabled()`); the price IDs must be pinned and the gate flipped from "billing off, everyone active" to live.
4. **In-product first-value under 5 minutes.** The audit *is* the sub-5-minute value (instant score); activation of the first workflow catching a first real lead is the sub-day value. The digest's TTFV<7-days-cuts-churn-50% rule is satisfied by design.

Where marginal cost approaches zero: **the second account on any workflow costs the OAuth handshake and a config row** — no build, no Jacob, no per-client repo. The governance engine (`decideAiContentGovernance` in `src/lib/ai-governance.ts`) is already a pure decision function that scales horizontally — it doesn't care if there are 6 tenants or 60,000. The audit caches results per URL. The receipt is generated, not written. The only residual human cost is the *review queue* — and that is bounded per-account, falls as templates harden, and is the thing the owner (not Jacob) staffs at scale.

## The pivot required — HONEST: what about today's bespoke/free/local/services model must change or be abandoned.

This frame is **not compatible** with how Strelva delivers today. Naming the gap exactly, per the honest-surface rule:

1. **The hand-built per-client repo as the delivery model for new customers must be abandoned.** `DEFAULT_DELIVERY_MODEL = "custom_repo"` and "every paid client is a separate hand-built repo" is the binding constraint. It caps growth at Jacob's build capacity. For this approach, new customers activate *workflows* self-serve; nobody builds them a repo. The custom-repo model survives only as a **premium concierge tier** ("we'll set up your whole stack for you"), never as the growth engine. This is the single hardest change and the one the digest says is the prerequisite for any fast-MRR trajectory.

2. **Self-serve must be turned ON.** `SELF_SERVE_ENABLED=false` is the current default and the `/access-request → Jacob builds it` flow is the current funnel. That funnel *is* the services trap. It must flip to `audit → self-serve activation → card`. Auto-provisioning being gated off is the thing standing between Strelva and software economics.

3. **Billing must go live and stop short-circuiting.** `isBillingEnabled()` returns false and the gate treats every tenant as active. `STRIPE_SCAFFOLD_PRICE_ID` is intentionally unpinned. For a subscription business, this is the whole product — pin the prices, flip the gate, wire self-serve checkout.

4. **The free-site-as-wedge flips to free-tool-as-wedge.** Today the *site* is the free acquisition gift (and the Jacob-hours bottleneck). Here the *audit* is the free gift and the *workflows* are paid. The free hand-built site stops being the acquisition motion. (It can persist inside the concierge tier.)

5. **Local/Buffalo focus is relaxed to national.** The BNI-seat / corridor-association distribution physics that powered Combination A do not scale past WNY. They are replaced by the three product-led loops above. Buffalo becomes the *first dense proving ground for the catalog*, not the market.

What stays: the governed AI agent, the audit, the receipt, the `/api/v1` contract, the dashboard, the cross-tenant data. The product layer is mostly right; the *delivery and go-to-market layer* is what must change from "Jacob builds bespoke, free, local" to "owner self-serves productized, paid, national."

## Moat — what compounds and resists copying at scale.

- **Governance precedent library.** Every workflow shipped under auto/review/block adds tested decision rules earned from real production incidents across thousands of accounts. A new entrant has speed without the gate; the digest notes 45% of AI-generated code ships with vulnerabilities — the governed-execution claim is the thing they structurally cannot speed-run. This compounds with every account, not every build.
- **Cross-vertical, cross-tenant operational benchmark.** Once N thousand SMBs run the same four workflows, Strelva holds a national operational dataset no single-tool competitor can produce: "med-spas that turn on No-Show Recovery cut no-shows 28%." That number sells the next subscription, feeds the receipt's emotional payload, and is GEO/AEO citation-bait. Data-network moats compound; feature moats don't.
- **The audit's SEO/backlink cascade + programmatic pages.** Years of compounding domain authority and millions of indexed health-report pages are a distribution moat a fast-follower must spend years and links to match (HubSpot's 40,000 backlinks took 5 years).
- **The flywheel a pure-monitoring tool can't replicate.** The digest's sharpest point: Strelva *detects the leak and fixes it without the owner doing anything*. A monitoring competitor shows you the problem; the governed agent closes it. Bundling detection + governed remediation is the structural advantage no audit-only or monitor-only player has.
- **Expansion-driven NRR.** Four workflows + usage ceiling + AEO SKU means NRR ≥100% is achievable from the installed base alone — the compound the digest says separates fast trajectories. A competitor with one workflow has no expansion surface.

## Risks & kill signal.

- **Risk: the 80/20 split fails at national scale.** If the four workflows are 50% bespoke once you leave Buffalo trades (different CRMs, different compliance per state/vertical), self-serve activation collapses back into Jacob-hours and the catalog is a lie. **Early signal:** self-serve activation completion rate for the first workflow is below ~40%, or median activations require a human rescue.
- **Risk: governance review queue eats the owner alive.** If too many workflow steps route to review, the owner churns because the tool created work instead of removing it. **Early signal:** review-queue volume per account doesn't fall across the first weeks, or accounts that hit a review backlog churn faster.
- **Risk: commoditization.** Wix, Squarespace, Framer, and well-funded AEO players (Profound at $1B, Peec) can bolt on SMB automations. The digest names this as the real risk — *not* lack of demand. Strelva must live in the governance + remediation flywheel, not site-building.
- **Risk: connector sprawl.** "Configure, don't build" is only true if the template covers the dominant CRM/booking tools. Too many connectors = a services queue in disguise.
- **Hard kill signal:** after the audit→self-serve→workflow funnel is live nationally for 90 days, if (a) self-serve activation of one real workflow without human help is below the floor AND (b) NRR is under 100% because nobody expands past their first workflow — then the productization premise is false at scale, and the business should retreat to the capped-but-real Combination A local services model. That retreat is survivable; pretending the self-serve works when activation needs rescue is not.

## First move this week — a concrete, self-serve-leaning step on existing assets.

**Make the audit a public, no-signup, national front door with one workflow recommendation wired to a self-serve activation stub — and ship ONE workflow self-serve end-to-end.**

Concretely, in priority order, all on existing primitives:

1. Expose the existing `/api/audit/scan` behind a public no-auth marketing page (paste URL → A–F grade → named leaks). It's already SSRF-safe, rate-limited, and cached. Add the operational diagnostic line: map the lowest-scoring category to a workflow recommendation ("you're losing web leads → Web-Lead → CRM, $49/mo").
2. Add an email gate *after* the score renders (proof first, gate second — the HubSpot pattern), capturing the lead into the existing event store.
3. Build *one* hardened, configurable Web-Lead → CRM workflow as a self-serve activation: OAuth the owner's CRM, the governed agent (reusing `decideAiContentGovernance`) inserts leads with one step routed to review. No Jacob repo. Pin a `$49/mo` Stripe price and wire self-serve checkout (flip `isBillingEnabled` for this SKU).
4. Instrument the funnel end to end: audit-run → email-capture → checkout-start → activation-complete → first-lead-caught.

The single sharpest test of the whole thesis: **can one stranger, with zero Jacob involvement, paste a URL, see their leak, pay $49, connect their CRM, and watch a real lead get caught — in one sitting?** That one event proves the self-serve loop closes and the services trap is escapable. If it requires a human at any step, the pivot isn't done.

## Redesign implication — what the product + marketing surface becomes.

**Marketing surface — from "AI websites for local business" to "a store of self-serve fixes for what's leaking out of your business."**

- The hero is **the audit input box**, not a value-prop paragraph and not a template gallery: *"Paste your URL. See what's leaking out of your business."* The grade is the hook; the named leaks are the pitch; each leak links to the workflow product that fixes it. This is national, no-signup, instant.
- Below it: a **catalog of named workflow products**, each a one-line job ("Web-Lead → CRM — stop losing the leads your site already gets"), a fixed "$49/mo, live in 10 minutes," a 30-second demo, and the trust line "governed by the same AI engine that runs it — the AI does it, you approve it."
- Proof is national benchmark data and live receipts, not Buffalo logos: "SMBs who turned on No-Show Recovery cut no-shows 28%." A public **"State of SMB Lead Leakage 2026"** report is the earned-media + AEO citation magnet.
- One primary CTA everywhere: **run the audit**. Self-serve checkout, no demo booking, no `/access-request` form as the default path.

**Product surface — from a bespoke project tracker to a self-serve catalog/inventory OS.**

- The dashboard's organizing metaphor is **inventory, not engagement**: each account shows which workflow products are installed, their governance activity (auto/review/block counts), the cross-tenant benchmark for that workflow, and the next-leak upsell drawn from the owner's own data.
- The receipt becomes the **forwardable, badged, single-CTA artifact** — workflow lines ("9 leads caught, 2 no-shows recovered"), a benchmark line as emotional payload, and a "audit your own business free" footer that turns every share into top-of-funnel.
- Governance is **visible, branded UI** — the guardrail is the thing being bought, so the owner watches auto/review/block work. That visible gate is the moat made legible.
- The concierge / hand-built tier appears as an **honestly-gated premium surface** ("done-for-you setup — coming soon / by request"), never as the default and never claimed as included in the self-serve price.
