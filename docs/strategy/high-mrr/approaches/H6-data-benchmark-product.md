# The Index — a national AI-visibility benchmark for local business

**One-liner** — Scan millions of *unsigned* local-business sites to build the only national, vertical-segmented benchmark of how businesses actually perform in AI search ("does ChatGPT recommend you?"), publish a public rank page for every business as programmatic-SEO bait, and convert worried owners into a $39–$99/mo self-serve monitoring subscription — the data network effect is the moat, the index is the lead engine, and being *the cited source* is the PR flywheel.

> This is the high-MRR re-optimization of the prior local benchmark approaches (`approaches/09-network-marketplace.md`, `approaches/12-benchmark-data-asset.md`). Those scoped the same data-network insight to Western New York and fed it through a free hand-built-site funnel. The thinness verdict in `SYNTHESIS.md` was correct *for a local managed-tenant dataset* — n=6 is anecdote. This approach removes the binding constraint that made it thin and slow: it stops waiting to *sign and build sites* to get data points and instead *scans the open web at national scale*, which is the only version of the benchmark that is both statistically dense on day one and nationally large. The benchmark is no longer exhaust from a services business — it *is* the product.

## The bet (high-MRR thesis) — why this can grow FAST and BIG, not slow and local.

The non-consensus wager: **the asset Strelva is accidentally sitting on is not the managed sites — it is a URL-in, score-out scanning engine that needs zero customers to produce data.** Every other benchmark in this space (Peec, Profound, Otterly, Semrush) is built from *signed customers' tracked prompts* — they can only benchmark the brands paying them, so their "industry average" is thin, paid, and enterprise-skewed. Strelva can scan any of the ~33M US local businesses *without signing a single one*, because the audit engine already takes an arbitrary URL and returns a graded result (`runAudit` in `src/lib/audit/checks.ts`). That asymmetry is the whole bet: **the denominator is free to build, and the denominator is the moat.**

Why it grows fast and big, not slow and local:

- **The TAM is national from line one.** ~33M US local businesses, each with the one question no single-vertical tool answers for them: "am I winning or losing against businesses like mine — and am I even visible when AI answers a customer's question?" Even 0.1% of US local businesses at $60/mo = $24M ARR (research digest). This is not a Buffalo wedge; the geographic constraint that capped the prior approach is deleted.
- **The category is provably willing to pay, right now.** Peec AI went $4M→$10M ARR in 6 months and crossed a $100M valuation; Profound raised $96M at a $1B valuation on ~$6.8M ARR; the AI-visibility category has $200M+ disclosed funding (digest). The *enterprise* tier is funded and crowded — but every named player targets marketing teams at mid-market brands. **The $39–$99/mo self-serve SMB-local tier is structurally unserved** (Otterly Lite at $29/15-prompts is the closest, and it has no local-business framing). Strelva can be the first credible "does ChatGPT recommend my plumbing business?" product.
- **The urgency curve is steep and in our favor.** AI Overviews cover ~48% of Google queries (up from 34.5% in Dec 2025); Gartner projects traditional search volume drops 25% by 2026; AI-search traffic converts at 14.2% vs 2.8% for organic (digest). Every quarter, more of an owner's customers find them through an answer the owner can't see. The fear is new, growing, and unaddressed at the SMB tier — that is the fastest-converting kind of fear.
- **AI-native compounds faster at every stage.** AI-native startups grow ~100% median vs 75% sub-$1M ARR and are 8x more likely to hit $10M ARR in 12 months (digest). This is an AI-native data product, not a CMS with AI bolted on.

The honest framing: this is a **data + monitoring SaaS**, not an agency. The prior approaches asked "how do we get more local clients to feed the benchmark?" This one asks "how do we make the benchmark so large and cited that businesses come to us to see their own rank?" — and answers it with scanning, not selling.

## MRR mechanics — pricing model, ACV, who pays, expansion revenue, and a realistic ramp.

**Who pays.** The owner (or marketer) of a local/SMB business who has just learned, from their own free rank page, that they are invisible or losing in AI search. Secondary buyer (the $100k+ accelerant): agencies and franchise groups who want to monitor and report AI-visibility for their whole client book.

**Pricing model — subscription floor + usage expansion (the NRR engine).** Usage-based hybrid is the documented fast-grower shape: 10% higher NRR, 22% lower churn vs flat-rate, and NRR ≥100% companies grow 2x faster YoY (digest). Concretely:

- **Free** — one full rank report per domain, public, no signup. This is the PLG wedge, not a tier to monetize. It is the ad.
- **Watch — $39/mo** — monitor *one* business across a fixed set of intent-matched prompts (e.g. 20 "best [trade] in [city]" / "[service] near me" queries), tracked weekly across ChatGPT + Google AI Overviews + Perplexity, with a rank-trend line and a forwardable weekly "AI-Visibility Receipt." This is the SMB self-serve floor.
- **Grow — $99/mo** — more prompts, more engines, competitor tracking ("you vs the 3 shops above you"), and **the fix layer**: the governed AI agent generates the JSON-LD/entity/content patches that close the gap (this is the asset no pure-monitoring tool has — see Leverage).
- **Agency — $299–$799/mo** — white-label, multi-client dashboard, per-sub-tenant rate (Vendasta/Duda range, digest). Expansion is *per managed business*, so a single agency seat expands like a usage meter.
- **Usage expansion across all paid tiers:** prompts tracked, engines monitored, and competitor slots are the meters. Heavy users (multi-location, multi-vertical) expand naturally; this is where NRR >100% comes from.

**ACV.** Self-serve SMB blended ~$50/mo → ~$600 ACV. Agency blended ~$450/mo → ~$5,400 ACV, expanding with each client they load. The agency tier is the ACV-per-logo multiplier that bends the curve.

**Realistic ramp (each milestone names what it requires):**

- **$0 → $10k MRR (target: 3–6 months).** ~200 Watch subs *or* ~30 agency seats. **Requires:** (1) the scan engine wired to LLM prompt-tracking (the one real new capability — run intent prompts against ChatGPT/Perplexity APIs, capture citation presence); (2) a persistent per-domain results store (does **not** exist yet — today's engine only 1-hour-caches a scan); (3) Stripe self-serve checkout + metering (billing is currently gated off — `isBillingEnabled()` returns false); (4) a public free rank page that converts to paid. This is the digest's stated "$10k MRR in 3–6 months with distribution behind it" path.
- **$10k → $100k MRR (target: +9–15 months).** ~1,500 self-serve subs + ~80 agency seats, NRR >100% doing real work. **Requires:** programmatic-SEO rank pages indexed at scale (the organic loop below) so signups arrive without spend; the fix layer live (governed agent closes gaps) to justify $99 and cut churn; agency white-label proven with 5+ reference partners. This is where the index *being cited by ChatGPT/Perplexity itself* starts compounding inbound.
- **$100k → $1M MRR (target: +12–24 months).** National vertical coverage (top 30 local verticals × top 200 metros indexed), agency channel as the dominant growth vector (one partner = dozens of sub-tenants), and the published "State of AI Visibility" benchmark as a recurring earned-media + sales asset. **Requires:** the benchmark to be the recognized *source* (citations, press, links) so CAC stays near zero at scale, and the data-network moat to be wide enough that a new entrant faces a multi-quarter cold-start. This is the Peec/Profound trajectory shape ($4M→$10M in 6 months once the category and motion click), at the unserved SMB price point they skip.

## Growth loop — the organic/PLG/viral engine (each user brings the next); time-to-traction.

Four compounding loops, all organic, no cold outbound:

1. **The free rank page (HubSpot Website Grader mechanic).** Owner enters URL → instant A–F AI-visibility grade + "you're invisible in 14 of 20 buyer questions" → email gate after the proof → upsell to weekly monitoring. Website Grader graded 4M sites and generated 40k+ organic backlinks as HubSpot's top lead-gen tool (digest). Strelva's engine is the same shape and already takes any URL. **Time-to-traction: immediate for lead-gen, 6–18 months for the SEO compounding.**
2. **Programmatic SEO at national scale (Zapier mechanic).** Auto-generate a public, indexable "AI-Visibility Report for [Business Name] — [City] [Vertical]" page for *every business we scan* — millions of long-tail pages capturing "[business name] reviews / website / AI visibility" and "[city] [trade] AI search" queries nationally. Each page is statistics-dense and citation-shaped, so **the index itself gets cited by ChatGPT/Perplexity** for local-business-visibility questions (GEO/AEO compounding — 60%+ of 2026 searches involve an AI component, digest). Pages start ranking in 3–6 months and compound for years.
3. **The forwardable receipt + "Powered by" output (ClickFunnels mechanic).** Every weekly AI-Visibility Receipt and every public rank page carries a "see your own AI rank — by Strelva" footer. ClickFunnels attributed ~$1M+/mo MRR (~20% of total) to a badge alone (digest). Each owner who shares a good rank to their network, or a marketer who sends a client report, is a top-of-funnel impression at zero marginal cost.
4. **The benchmark-as-PR flywheel.** Publish "State of AI Visibility for US Local Business" with real distributions ("78% of US plumbers are invisible when ChatGPT is asked to recommend one"). Statistics-dense, named-methodology content is exactly what LLMs cite and what trade press runs. Being *the source* makes Strelva the default answer to "how do I check my AI visibility" — a positioning a competitor cannot copy without the dataset.

**No cold outbound anywhere.** The product output (rank page, receipt, benchmark stat) is the distribution. The bottleneck is engineering the wow moment ("you are invisible in 14 of 20 questions your customers ask AI"), not finding a channel.

## Why it scales (software economics) — where marginal cost approaches zero; what is automated.

The whole point of this approach is that **no step is founder-hour-bound.** Marginal cost per additional benchmarked business approaches zero:

- **Scanning is automated and cheap.** `runAudit` already takes a URL and returns a graded result with SSRF protection, parallelized checks, and result caching. Adding businesses to the index is a crawl job, not a sale. The marginal cost of one more data point is an API call.
- **LLM prompt-tracking is metered, not labored.** Running 20 intent prompts against ChatGPT/Perplexity per monitored business is a scheduled cron job (Strelva already runs polling crons — `src/app/api/cron/`). Model cost is the only variable cost and is a controllable fraction of a $39–$99 sub.
- **Onboarding is zero-touch.** Self-serve checkout → pick your business (already scanned) → monitoring starts. No Jacob, no repo build, no human step. This is the flip the digest names as the prerequisite for software margins (70–80% vs 30–50% services).
- **The fix layer is the governed AI agent, not a human.** When a gap is detected, the existing agent (`src/lib/agent-executor.ts`) drafts the JSON-LD/content patch under governance (`src/lib/ai-governance.ts`). Auto-approve factual fixes, queue copy for review. Founder hours scale with *exceptions*, not with customer count.
- **Programmatic pages generate themselves** from scan data. National coverage is a data-generation job, not a content team.

Contrast with today: every current Strelva customer costs Jacob a hand-built repo. Here, the 10,000th monitored business costs the same as the 10th. That is the services→software flip, made concrete.

## The pivot required — HONEST: what must change or be abandoned.

This requires real structural change. Naming it plainly, because the founder asked:

- **Abandon the free hand-built custom site as the acquisition wedge.** The wedge becomes the *free scan/rank page*, not a free site. Jacob building repos is the binding constraint that caps the prior model at his capacity; this approach cannot use it as the growth engine. Hand-built sites survive only as an optional premium/concierge tier, *not* the funnel.
- **Abandon the local/Buffalo geographic constraint as the strategy.** The whole MRR thesis is national TAM. Buffalo can be the *seed dataset and the first PR story* ("State of Buffalo Trades AI Visibility"), but the company's identity stops being "the Buffalo studio" and becomes "the AI-visibility index for local business." The prior synthesis's Buffalo-as-non-portable-amplifier advantage is deliberately traded away for ceiling.
- **Build three things that do not exist today.** (1) **A persistent per-domain results store** — the current engine only 1-hour-caches a scan and per-IP rate-limits; there is no longitudinal index. This is the data asset; it must be built. (2) **LLM prompt-tracking** — running intent prompts against ChatGPT/Perplexity and capturing citation presence does not exist yet; it is the one genuinely new capability and the core differentiator. (3) **Self-serve billing + metering** — `isBillingEnabled()` returns false and checkout is gated off; the pricing thesis requires zero-touch Stripe self-serve with usage metering.
- **Shift the product identity from "AI website manager" to "AI-visibility data product."** The dashboard's hero stops being "see what's working on your site" and becomes "your AI-visibility rank and trend." The CMS/content-agent becomes the *fix layer underneath the monitoring*, not the headline.

What carries over and de-risks the pivot: the scan engine, the governed agent, the versioned contract, the cron backbone, and the receipt are all reused. This is not a rewrite — it is a re-pointing of existing assets at a national data product, plus three named builds.

## Moat — what compounds and resists copying at scale.

- **Data network effect (the durable one).** Every scanned business widens the denominator; every monitored business adds longitudinal trend data. A new entrant must scan and re-scan the same national universe *over time* to match the distributions and the "your rank dropped" history — a multi-quarter cold-start against a moving target. This is the vertical-SaaS moat that the prior local version couldn't reach at n=6; national scanning gets there on day one.
- **Citation/source authority (compounds with the data).** As the index gets cited by ChatGPT/Perplexity and trade press for "AI visibility of local business," Strelva becomes the *default answer* — a self-reinforcing GEO moat that funded enterprise tools (which don't publish a public SMB benchmark) structurally don't build.
- **The fix layer (the asymmetry vs pure monitors).** Detect-and-fix-under-governance is a flywheel no monitoring-only competitor replicates without building a governed content agent on a live delivery contract — which Strelva already runs.
- **Programmatic-SEO surface area.** Millions of indexed rank pages are a backlink-and-discovery moat that compounds for years and is expensive to out-publish.

## Risks & kill signal.

- **LLM prompt-tracking is noisy and costly.** Answers vary by run; citation presence is probabilistic; API costs scale with monitored prompts. **Mitigation:** track *share-of-citation over N runs* not single answers; cap prompt counts per tier as the usage meter. **Kill signal:** if model cost per sub exceeds ~20% of price at scale, or owners reject the metric as unreliable, the monitoring product is unsound — fall back to the deterministic web-presence/AEO-readiness index (still real, less hot).
- **Enterprise incumbents move down-market.** Peec/Profound are funded and could ship an SMB tier. **Mitigation:** win the unserved $39–$99 self-serve niche fast and bolt on the fix layer they don't have. **Kill signal:** a funded player ships a credible <$50/mo local product before Strelva has data-moat density.
- **"Interesting but won't pay."** Owners may find the rank alarming but not subscribe. **Kill signal (the real one):** if free-scan → paid conversion is materially below the 2–5% freemium median after the funnel is live, the fear isn't monetizable at this price — re-test pricing or pivot the buyer to agencies (who provably pay, digest).
- **Anonymization/trust on the public index.** Publishing per-business rank pages for unsigned businesses must be defensible (public data, clearly sourced) or it invites takedown/PR risk. **Mitigation:** aggregate distributions public; per-business pages use only publicly observable signals.
