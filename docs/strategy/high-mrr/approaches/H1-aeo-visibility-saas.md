# AnswerRank — Self-Serve AI-Visibility Monitor

**One-liner** — Turn Strelva's any-URL audit engine into a national, self-serve SaaS that answers one urgent question for every business on earth — *"Does ChatGPT recommend you?"* — for free in 30 seconds, then charges $39–$199/month to monitor and fix it as the answer keeps changing. The free scan is the ad; the recurring drift of AI answers is the reason the subscription never ends.

> This is the high-MRR, fast-growth re-optimization of approach 08 (Answer-Native Buffalo). 08 was the right *wedge* aimed at the wrong *ceiling*: it bolted AEO onto a local, hand-built, free-site services motion that caps at Jacob's build capacity. This frame keeps the wedge, drops the local/services/free-site constraints, and makes the monitor itself the product that scales nationally with near-zero marginal cost. Where it requires abandoning Strelva's current shape, the **The pivot required** section says so explicitly.

## The bet (high-MRR thesis)

The fastest-growing software companies of 2024–2026 share one structural trait: a **free output artifact that users compulsively show other people**, attached to a **broad horizontal TAM**, sold **zero-touch self-serve**. Cursor ($0→$1B ARR in ~24 months, zero marketing spend, 36% freemium conversion vs. 2–5% industry median) and Lovable ($0→$17M ARR in 3 months, $400M in 14 months, 15 employees, no paid ads) are the benchmark outliers, but they reveal the lever: the product *is* the distribution engine, not the funnel. Vertical SMB SaaS structurally caps out — funeral-home software hit $29k MRR in 12 months and that was the ceiling, not the floor. Horizontal won every $400M+ case.

The bet is that **AI-search visibility monitoring is the rare category where a free, instantly-shareable, score-shaped artifact and an urgent, recurring, horizontal job line up perfectly — and the SMB tier is wide open right now.** The category's willingness-to-pay is already proven at scale at the enterprise tier: **Peec AI went $4M→$10M ARR in six months** (Nov 2025, valuation past $100M on a $21M Series A); **Profound raised $96M at a $1B valuation** (Feb 2026) on ~$6.8M ARR; **Bluefish raised a $43M Series B** (Apr 2026); the category has $200M+ in disclosed funding. Every one of those tools targets marketing teams at Wix-, Ramp-, DocuSign-scale brands. **No one has a credible $39/month self-serve product built for a plumber, a dentist, a yoga studio, or a Shopify store asking "does ChatGPT name me?"** Otterly's $29/month Lite (15 prompts) is the closest, and it is a generic prompt-tracker, not a fix-it loop.

Why this grows fast and big rather than slow and local:
- **The job is universal and newly urgent.** AI Overviews now cover ~48% of Google queries (up from 34% in Dec 2025); Gartner projects traditional search volume drops 25% by 2026; AI-referred traffic converts at ~14.2% vs. ~2.8% for Google organic. Every business with a web presence has a reason to care — that is national+ TAM, not one city. US local businesses alone number ~33M; **0.1% penetration at $60/month average = $24M ARR.**
- **The answer keeps drifting, so the monitor is inherently recurring.** Unlike a one-time SEO audit, "are you in the AI answer?" changes every time a model is retrained, a competitor publishes, or a prompt phrasing shifts. The thing you're measuring is non-stationary — that is the structural reason this is a subscription and not a one-off report. Churn fights itself.
- **The window is open and closing.** The enterprise players are racing up-market toward six-figure ACVs; none has turned to build a true self-serve SMB tier. First credible self-serve product in the SMB slot owns the category's long tail before they localize down.

This is AI-native, which the data says grows 2–3x faster at every stage (AI-native <$1M ARR grows at 100% median vs. 75% traditional; $1M–$5M at 110% vs. 40%; 8x more likely to reach $10M ARR in 12 months). It rides an existing, working asset (the audit engine is ~70% of the infrastructure). And it is horizontal, so the ceiling is the AI-search-optimization market — $3.2B in 2026 → $17–31B by 2033–2035 at 20–26% CAGR — not a single metro.

## MRR mechanics

**Pricing model — flat floor + usage expansion (hybrid).** Usage-based SaaS delivers ~10% higher NRR and ~22% lower churn than flat-rate, and NRR ≥100% is the single compound that makes companies grow 2x faster YoY. The model is a flat recurring floor priced per *property/brand* with expansion metered on *tracked prompts, competitors, and locations*:

| Tier | Price | Who | What's metered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Free scan** | $0, no signup | Anyone with a URL | 1 instant AI-visibility grade, 3 prompts sampled live, public OG share card |
| **Track** | $39/mo | Solo SMB / one location | 1 brand, 15 tracked prompts, weekly re-scan, ChatGPT + Google AI Overviews, fix checklist |
| **Grow** | $99/mo | Multi-location SMB, agencies' small clients | 3 brands, 50 prompts, +Perplexity +Gemini, competitor tracking, fix-it AI agent, alerting |
| **Pro** | $199/mo | Franchise, serious local brand | 10 brands, 150 prompts, all engines, API access, white-label report PDF |
| **Agency / white-label** | $299–$799/mo | Agencies, franchise groups | 20–100 sub-tenants metered at $15–25/each, reseller dashboard |

Anchors are real: Otterly $29→$489, Visiblie €79–€199, Peec €89–€199/project, Hall $199–$1,499, Semrush AI-visibility add-on $99, Duda white-label $199. Strelva's SMB tier sits *below* every incumbent's entry point on purpose — the free scan does the selling, the price removes the decision.

**ACV.** Blended self-serve ACV ~$700–$1,200/yr ($60–$100 blended MRR/account). Agency accounts ~$3,600–$9,600/yr and bring 20–100 sub-tenants each — the expansion multiplier.

**Who pays, and why they keep paying.** The free scan creates the problem awareness ("you're invisible in ChatGPT for 'best [trade] in [city]'"); the paid tier is the only way to (a) watch the score over time, (b) get alerted when a competitor overtakes them in the answer, and (c) have the **fix-it AI agent** generate the schema/FAQ/entity content that moves the number — closing the loop no pure-monitoring tool closes. They keep paying because the answer drifts and a competitor is always one prompt away from taking their slot.

**Expansion revenue (the NRR engine).** Land at $39 (one brand, 15 prompts) → expand on (1) more prompts as they discover queries that matter, (2) more competitors tracked, (3) more locations/brands, (4) more engines, (5) the fix-it agent moving from suggestion to done-for-you. Agency accounts expand by adding sub-tenants. Target NRR ≥110% — that is the difference between the fast trajectories and the median.

**Realistic ramp.**
- **$10k MRR (months 3–6).** ~140–170 paid accounts at a $60–70 blended MRR. Requires: free scan live and frictionless at strelva.com (or a clean standalone domain), email capture after the score reveal, a self-serve Stripe checkout with no human in the loop, weekly re-scan cron, and the OG share card. This is reachable on the existing audit engine + the existing Stripe/Redis/cron infra **with zero new sales motion** — the digest's "3–6 months with distribution behind it" estimate. The binding input is *traffic to the free scan*, solved by the growth loop below, not headcount.
- **$100k MRR (months 12–24).** ~1,200–1,500 accounts blended, *with* the agency channel contributing the multiplier: 30–60 agency accounts at $300–800 each bringing thousands of sub-tenant scans into the funnel. Requires: programmatic SEO pages indexing at national scale, the fix-it agent proven to move scores (so the share artifacts say "we fixed it," not just "here's your problem"), and NRR ≥105% so expansion compounds. This is where the local/services model *must* already be abandoned — at 1,500 self-serve accounts there cannot be a Jacob-build step anywhere in activation.
- **$1M MRR ($12M ARR, months 30–48).** Top-decile but on-trajectory for AI-native (3.3% of SaaS reach $1M ARR <12mo; AI-native 8x likelier to reach $10M ARR in 12mo). Requires the data moat to be live (cross-tenant AI-visibility benchmarks by vertical/geo become the product's spine and the citation magnet), the white-label channel to be a real reseller business, and at least one engine (the fix-it agent) productized into done-for-you so heavy accounts expand into $199–$799 tiers. This milestone is a platform, not a tool — it needs the benchmark dataset that only accumulates with volume, which is why the first two milestones must run the data flywheel from day one.

## Growth loop

The engine is **output virality + free-grader SEO + programmatic pages**, three loops that compound on each other. No cold outbound — the digest is explicit that cold email is dead in 2026.

**Loop 1 — the score is the ad (output virality).** HubSpot's Website Grader graded 4M sites and generated 40,000+ organic backlinks and became its top lead-gen tool; ClickFunnels attributed $1M+/month MRR (~20% of total) to a "Powered by" badge alone. Strelva's audit already emits an A–F grade — the share mechanic wraps it: every free scan produces a **public OG card** ("[Business] scores D on AI visibility — see who ChatGPT recommends instead") and a **public result page** the owner forwards to their web person, their marketing agency, or posts to defend/brag. Each share is a top-of-funnel impression on a non-user carrying "powered by Strelva." The psychological hook (shame/pride of a letter grade) is the same one the engine already exploits, pointed at a newly visible, newly scary gap. Time-to-traction: lead-gen immediate, SEO backlink cascade compounds over 6–18 months.

**Loop 2 — programmatic product-led SEO (Zapier model).** Auto-generate a public **"[Business Name] AI Visibility Report"** page for indexed businesses by pulling the data the scan already produces, nationally. Millions of long-tail pages capture "[business] ChatGPT review" / "[city] [trade] AI visibility" queries. These pages are statistics-dense and citation-shaped, so they get cited *by ChatGPT/Perplexity themselves* (GEO/AEO compounding) — the product becomes the answer to its own category's queries. Starts ranking in 3–6 months, compounds with Loop 1's domain authority.

**Loop 3 — the agency smuggle (bottom-up to the white-label channel).** Individual freelancers and agency owners use the free scan to *prospect their own clients* ("your site is invisible in AI, here's proof") — the tool does their sales for them. Heavy free users self-identify; the upsell is the white-label tier that lets them resell monitoring to their whole book. One agency signing = 20–100 SMBs into the funnel. This is the Vendasta multiplier ($92.9M ARR, 66,000 agency partners, 8.2M SMBs) reached organically, not via a sales team.

**Each user brings the next:** owner runs free scan → gets a scary grade + share card → forwards it / posts it → recipient runs their own scan → the programmatic page for both ranks and gets cited by AI → more strangers find the scan. Paid conversion happens where the drift becomes anxiety (a competitor overtakes them; the score drops after a model update) — the monitor sells itself on its own recurring bad news.

## Why it scales (software economics)

Marginal cost per additional tenant approaches zero, which is the whole point of the pivot:
- **A scan is an API call, not a build.** `runAudit()` fetches HTML, parses it with cheerio, calls Google PageSpeed, samples LLM prompts. The added AEO layer is LLM-prompt sampling against the ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini APIs — pure API cost, cents per scan, cached in Redis (`reb:audit:` keys, already 1-hour cached) and rate-limited (already 3/day/IP). No human touches a scan.
- **Activation is self-serve and instant.** Free scan → score reveal → email gate → Stripe checkout → recurring cron does the re-scans. The digest's "four automations required to flip margins" — zero-touch onboarding, self-serve activation without a custom repo, automated billing/metering, in-product first-value under 5 minutes — are *all* achievable here because there is no site to build. First value is the score, delivered in 30 seconds, before signup.
- **The fix-it agent is the only "labor," and it's automated.** Strelva's governed AI agent generates the schema/FAQ/entity content that moves the score. Governance (auto-approve factual / review copy / block structural) is exactly the safety layer that lets the agent suggest fixes at scale without a human reviewing every one. Model cost stays a single-digit % of revenue because suggestions are cheap (Gemini Flash) and gated.
- **Cron does the recurring work.** The weekly re-scan, alerting, and "your score changed" emails ride the existing cron infrastructure (`vercel.json` crons, `weekly-brief.ts`, `reports.ts`) — fan-out over N tenants is O(tenants) compute, not O(founder-hours).

ARR-per-FTE target is the 2022+ AI-native benchmark ($1M–$2M/FTE), which is only reachable because no founder hour is spent per customer. That is the explicit break from today's model.

## Leverage from Strelva assets

This rides more existing, working code than any other approach — it is an *extraction*, the Basecamp/Shopify pattern (internal tool → product), not a greenfield build:

- **Audit engine (`src/lib/audit/checks.ts`, `scoring.ts`, `/api/audit/scan/route.ts`, `/audit` page).** ~70% of the product already exists: weighted A–F scoring, JSON-LD/schema parsing, SSRF-safe URL validation (IPv4-forced, DNS-rebinding guarded), Redis result caching, IP rate-limiting, and a live public scan page. The four exported AEO stubs (`stubGBPCompleteness`, `stubNAPConsistency`, `stubReviewPresence`, `stubLocalSEOGrid`) are pre-built slots for the local-AEO checks; the new work is one category — live LLM-prompt sampling — not a new engine.
- **Governed AI agent (`src/lib/agent-executor.ts`, `src/lib/ai-governance.ts`).** Becomes the **fix-it agent** that turns a low score into applied content fixes, with governance as the at-scale safety layer. This is the differentiator pure monitors (Peec, Otterly, Profound) structurally lack — they tell you the problem; Strelva fixes it.
- **`/api/v1` versioned contract + signed HMAC revalidation (`src/lib/scaffold-contracts.ts`, `revalidate-client.ts`).** Already the right shape for the white-label/agency channel — sub-tenant content delivery and signed updates are solved.
- **Per-tenant event/review/social data store + integrations (Google/Yelp/Instagram/Search Console, `src/lib/integration-registry.ts`, `connections.ts`).** Feeds the GBP/NAP/review AEO checks the stubs anticipate, and the cross-tenant benchmark dataset.
- **Weekly receipt infra (`weekly-brief.ts`, `reports.ts`) + cron.** Becomes the recurring "your AI-visibility score this week" email — the retention anchor and a Loop-1 share surface (footer: "scan your own site").
- **Stripe + subscription gating (`src/lib/subscription.ts`).** `isBillingEnabled()` already gates on a configured price; turning on self-serve billing is flipping a price ID and the gate, not building billing.

## The pivot required

Honest: **Strelva today is structurally a services/agency business** — hand-built per-client custom repos, free client sites as the acquisition wedge, founder-hour-bound delivery, local Buffalo focus. None of that survives contact with this approach. What must change or be abandoned:

1. **The free hand-built custom site stops being the acquisition wedge.** It is replaced by the **free audit/scan as the wedge.** This is the central flip the digest names: *free-site-as-acquisition* → *free-tool-as-acquisition*. The hand-built site can survive only as an optional premium concierge tier ("we'll fix everything for you"), never as the growth engine — every Jacob-build is a hard cap on velocity.
2. **`DEFAULT_DELIVERY_MODEL = "custom_repo"` and the whole custom-repo delivery pipeline are no longer on the activation path.** A paying AnswerRank customer onboards with zero Jacob involvement: scan → checkout → monitoring. The custom-repo machinery becomes relevant only if a customer upgrades to the concierge "fix my whole site" tier. `SELF_SERVE_ENABLED` flips from gated-off to on for the monitor product.
3. **Local/Buffalo focus is dropped for the monitor.** The product is national/horizontal from day one. Buffalo trades can be the *first dogfood dataset* (run the scan across known local businesses to seed the benchmark and the programmatic pages), but the TAM, positioning, and growth loop are explicitly not geo-bound. The "City of Good Neighbors" trust moat does not transfer to a national SaaS — and that is fine, because the moat moves to data (below).
4. **The monetized object changes.** Today the bet is "owner pays for a dashboard proving their hand-built site works." Here the bet is "anyone with any website pays to be visible to AI." The dashboard, weekly receipt, and governed agent survive and get *sharper* — they just attach to a self-serve monitor instead of a bespoke site.
5. **Pricing must be set and self-serve now.** Today billing is off (`isBillingEnabled()` returns false, every tenant treated active). That is incompatible with a PLG SaaS. A real price (the $39 floor) and self-serve Stripe checkout are prerequisites, not later steps.

What survives unchanged: the audit engine, the governed agent, the `/api/v1` + HMAC contract, the data store, the receipt, and the cron infra. The pivot is in the *go-to-market shape and the delivery model*, not the technology — which is exactly why it's reachable.

## Moat

Pure AI-visibility monitoring is crowded at the enterprise tier and the checks themselves are copyable. The defensible compounding lives in three places no pure-monitor and no incumbent racing up-market holds:

1. **The fix-it loop, not the measurement.** Peec/Profound/Otterly tell you you're invisible. Strelva's governed agent *fixes it* — generates the schema, FAQ, and entity content, applies it (for managed sites) or hands a copy-paste patch (for monitor-only), and proves the score climbing in the receipt. Detect-and-fix in one governed product is a flywheel a monitoring tool cannot bolt on without building Strelva's agent + governance from scratch.
2. **Cross-tenant AI-visibility benchmark data.** Every free scan and every paid tenant feeds a national, by-vertical, by-geo dataset of *what AI-visibility scores actually look like and what moves them*. After volume, "dentists in your metro average 62; the one fix that moved three of them is X" is a number no single-tenant tool can produce — and it's the citation-magnet content (Loop 2) that gets Strelva cited by the very AI engines it measures. The data compounds with every scan, paid or free; competitors copying the checks don't get the dataset.
3. **The proof-loop on operated surfaces.** For customers who also let the agent apply fixes, Strelva re-scores continuously and owns the before/after history — switching means losing the proof trail. A competitor's audit is a snapshot; Strelva's is a controlled, improving, *proven* time series.

The honest read: moat 1 and 3 are real and Strelva-specific today; moat 2 is the durable one but must be *earned with volume*, not claimed at low n. The defensibility against well-funded incumbents (Profound at $1B) is not out-monitoring them — it's owning the SMB long tail they're structurally ignoring, with a fix-it loop they don't have.

## Risks & kill signal

- **The job isn't felt yet at SMB scale.** Enterprise marketers feel AI-invisibility; a plumber may not. **Kill signal:** free-scan → paid conversion stays below ~2% after 5k+ scans with the share/email funnel fully wired — meaning the grade scares people but the subscription doesn't close. Fallback: pivot the wedge to the agency channel (who feel it on behalf of clients) before pivoting the product.
- **Citation/score mechanics are noisier than the score implies.** If a higher AI-visibility score doesn't produce measurably more citations within 60–90 days, the fix-it loop's marquee claim is hollow. **Kill signal:** fixes show no measurable AI-citation lift across the first cohort over a quarter → demote the fix-it claim to "structured-data hygiene," keep monitoring as the product.
- **Incumbents drop down-market.** Profound/Peec ship a $39 self-serve SMB tier. **Survivable** only via the fix-it loop + benchmark data + the free-grader distribution they don't run; **watch** as a margin-compressor on the wedge. This is the real existential risk and the reason speed matters — the window is the moat until the data is.
- **Commoditization from Google/Wix.** Platforms add an "AI-ready" toggle. Survivable because a toggle isn't a monitor, a benchmark, or a fix-it agent — but it compresses the free-tool novelty.
- **Model API cost/ToS for prompt-sampling.** Sampling ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini at scale has cost and ToS exposure. **Mitigation:** cache aggressively (already built), batch, and use cheapest models for sampling; keep model cost a single-digit % of revenue as a hard gate.

**Marketing surface stops being "AI websites for local business" and becomes a single free tool.** The homepage *is* the scan input — *"Does ChatGPT recommend your business? Find out in 30 seconds."* The first experience is the live grade + the side-by-side of what AI says about you now vs. your tracked competitor. The category language moves from "AI website management" to **"AI-visibility monitoring"** / **"Be the answer, not just a result."** The site itself must be the most answer-native page in its category (it's the proof, and it must win the citation for "how do I show up in AI search"). One primary CTA (scan), one secondary (start monitoring — the paid tier), pricing public and self-serve. No "request access," no "Jacob builds your site."

The weekly receipt becomes the forwardable "your AI visibility this week" email with a "scan your own site" footer (Loop 1 referral). The governed agent surfaces as the **fix-it panel** — "3 fixes will move your score from D to B," with auto/review/block governance visible as the trust layer. The custom-repo "build" flow disappears from the self-serve path entirely and survives only behind an optional "have us fix it for you" concierge upsell.
