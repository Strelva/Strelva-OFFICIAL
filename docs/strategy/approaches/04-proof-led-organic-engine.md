# The Proof Engine

**One-liner** — Strelva stops marketing and instead ships proof artifacts — a live audit score, a public before/after scorecard, and a weekly plain-English receipt — engineered so that every output a client receives is simultaneously a piece of distribution that pulls the next client in, with zero cold outbound.

## The bet
The non-consensus wager is that for a two-person Buffalo studio, **the product and the marketing are the same artifact** — and that competitors who treat them as separate functions (build the site, then run ads) will lose on cost structure alone. Every credible local agency in 2026 is buying a separate audit widget, a separate SEO retainer, and a separate ad budget; Strelva already owns an audit engine (`/api/audit/scan`) and a weekly receipt generator (`src/lib/weekly-brief.ts`) that, if engineered to be *public and forwardable by default*, become a self-funding acquisition loop. The bet is right if proof beats persuasion in a market (local SMB) where the dominant emotion is not aspiration but loss-aversion and embarrassment — owners don't want to be sold a website, they want evidence theirs isn't silently failing. We make the evidence free, public, and benchmarked, so the act of proving value to one client is the act of acquiring the next.

## Jobs-To-Be-Done served
- **Functional job:** "Tell me, in plain language, whether my web presence is working — and fix it without handing me a tool to manage." The audit answers the diagnostic half ("here's your score and what's broken"); the managed site + weekly receipt answers the ongoing half ("here's who found you this week"). The owner never has to open GA4, which they admit they never do.
- **Emotional job:** Remove the ambient anxiety that the site is quietly dead. The receipt is *proof of life* — "47 people found you, 6 clicked your booking link" — not analytics. The audit, delivered cold, names a fear the owner already half-feels ("you're invisible in AI search") and immediately resolves it.
- **Social job:** Look real, established, and in-control to customers and peers before first contact. A benchmarked score the owner can show ("we score 82, the regional median is 61") is a credibility artifact they *want* to forward — which is exactly what makes it distribution.
- **Trigger / struggling moment:** Not "I want a new website." It's silent lead decay (30 leads/month in 2022 → 8 now, cause unknown), a customer saying "your site looks broken on my phone," or a competitor appearing in a Google AI Overview that the owner isn't cited in. The free audit is engineered to *land precisely at that moment* — the owner runs their own URL out of nagging worry, sees the number, and raises their hand. The rep becomes an advisor reacting to a self-diagnosed problem, never a cold caller.

## What Strelva becomes
Strelva becomes a **proof factory** whose three artifacts each do double duty as product and distribution:

1. **The Audit (top of funnel, acquisition).** Any-URL scorecard, gated by email, that scores the live site *and* — the non-consensus twist — shows a "this is what it would look like under Strelva" delta and a benchmark against the Buffalo tenant population. It is not a lead-magnet PDF; it is a demo disguised as a diagnosis.
2. **The Managed Site (middle, the free wedge — Noah's division).** Noah hand-builds the custom repo, free, and it ships *AEO-ready by default* (JSON-LD, GBP entity consistency) so the audit score the prospect just saw goes green the day they sign. The site is the delivery vehicle; it is not the thing being sold.
3. **The Receipt (bottom + viral loop, the monetized layer — Noah's division).** The weekly brief is redesigned to be **clean, branded, single-CTA, and built to forward.** It carries a cross-tenant benchmark line ("your booking clicks are 2.1x the WNY wellness median") that no single-tenant tool can produce, and a visible "Audit your own site" footer that turns every owner who shares their receipt into a top-of-funnel referrer.

**Noah (Websites)** owns the full loop above — audit → free site → receipt → referral. **Jacob (Custom Software)** plays the *expansion*, not the wedge: the audit engine and benchmark dashboard are themselves the proof that earns Jacob's credibility before a single workflow ships ("the studio that already runs a governed AI agent in production"). Jacob's artifact-equivalent is a **public "workflow teardown"** — a forwardable one-page diagnosis of a single repetitive process (estimate-to-invoice, intake routing) sourced from the same Buffalo relationships the receipts are already circulating through. Wedge = the free audit. Expansion = the receipt's benchmark line (which justifies the monetized dashboard) and, downstream, Jacob's one-workflow teardown to the trades firms the website loop already touches.

## Novel Q4-2026 capability exploited
The load-bearing new capability is **AEO/GEO scoring baked into the audit as a default category, not an upsell.** Twelve months ago this was not feasible as a *productized acquisition artifact*: AI search referral was a rounding error, the "AI Visibility" factors (structured-data completeness, GBP entity consistency, citation presence) were not yet the dominant local-visibility battleground, and there was no clean story connecting "your JSON-LD is incomplete" to "you are invisible in ChatGPT/Perplexity answers." By Q4 2026 the research is unambiguous: complete structured data earns 2.5x more AI Overview appearances, 78% of local trades are invisible in AI search, AI-referred traffic converts at ~14% vs ~3% for Google organic, and 51% of companies plan to *increase* AEO investment while only 20% have started — a first-mover window. Strelva's audit already fetches HTML and parses JSON-LD (`checkSchema` in `src/lib/audit/checks.ts`); the four Phase 2 stubs (`stubGBPCompleteness`, `stubNAPConsistency`, `stubReviewPresence`, `stubLocalSEOGrid`) map *exactly* onto AEO factors. Wiring them into a single "AI Visibility" score produces the only local-business audit that scores both traditional and AI-search health from the live production site — a number competitors literally cannot generate without also running the site. The second newly-possible thing: cross-tenant benchmarking only became *true* now that Strelva runs 6+ tenants on identical schemas — the receipt's "vs. comparable WNY businesses" line was a hollow claim at 1 client and is real data at 6.

## Organic GTM motion
The flywheel: **Audit (pull) → free AEO-ready site → forwardable benchmarked receipt → owner shares it / refers a peer → peer runs the audit.** Each loop costs Strelva near-zero marginal dollars because the artifacts are already built; the only variable cost is Noah's build time per signed client. No cold email, no ads — the audit is run by worried owners who found it, and the receipt is forwarded by happy ones who own it.

**Buffalo/WNY angle:** Buffalo is a *structured* referral graph with explicit on-ramps, and a forwardable proof artifact is the perfect payload for it. The "City of Good Neighbors" norm penalizes generic remote vendors and rewards embedded locals who stand behind their work — a benchmarked receipt delivered by a named Buffalo studio compounds trust faster here than anywhere. The construction boom (1,417 Erie County trades establishments, 35-year employment high) is a vertical drowning in work with stale 2018 websites — the audit's "you're invisible in AI search" cold-open lands hard on owners too busy to have noticed.

**First 3 concrete distribution acts (all organic):**
1. **Seed the audit into BNI WNY by taking the one "web/AI" category seat** — run live audits on members' sites at a chapter visit; the one-seat-per-category rule means owning that seat locks out competitors, and 20–40 aggressive referrers see their own score in real time.
2. **Publish anonymized vertical benchmarks as AEO content** ("Median AI-visibility score for Buffalo wellness studios is 54 — here's why") — this pulls prospects *and* feeds the AI-citation flywheel, making Strelva the answer when an owner asks an AI assistant "how's my local website doing?"
3. **Redesign the weekly receipt as a forwardable, single-CTA artifact** and pitch one Buffalo Rising / Buffalo Business First earned-media piece around a real before/after ("this Hertel-strip studio went from invisible to cited in AI search in 6 weeks") — earned media, not paid.

## Moat / where value compounds
Two things harden with every unit of use, and both are owned by the same loop. First, **the benchmark dataset**: every new tenant and every audit run (stored per-domain) deepens the cross-tenant population, making the receipt's "vs. comparable WNY businesses" line more precise and more impossible to replicate — a competitor at Strelva's tier would need to *also* run dozens of Buffalo sites on identical schemas to match it, which takes years they don't have. Second, **the referral graph itself**: in Buffalo's structured, one-seat-per-category network, each forwarded receipt and each chapter audit deposits trust into a relationship web that is geographically dense and culturally anti-transactional — the moat is not the software, it's the accumulated standing in a market where a referral is one phone call and a bad one is socially punished. The AEO-ready delivery pipeline compounds quietly underneath: every site Noah ships green raises the population's median, which makes the *next* prospect's stale site score worse by comparison, sharpening the audit's cold-open over time.

## Why competitors will not (or cannot) copy this
The consensus playbook treats audit, build, and retention as three separate cost centers — a bought audit widget, a white-label site platform, an SEO retainer, ad spend. Strelva collapses them into one artifact loop, which means **its CAC approaches zero while competitors' stays linear.** Three asymmetries the consensus misses: (1) **They sell the artifact; Strelva sells the evidence.** Every competitor ships "a website" or "analytics"; nobody closes the "is it working?" loop weekly in plain language designed to be forwarded — the receipt-as-referral-engine is structurally absent from the agency model. (2) **They cannot produce the benchmark.** A single-tenant tool, a DIY platform, and a one-off agency build all lack cross-customer data at the local vertical level; the "vs. comparable WNY businesses" line requires multi-tenancy they don't have. (3) **They cannot replicate Buffalo standing.** A geo-agnostic competitor running LinkedIn ads and cold email is playing the exact game the "City of Good Neighbors" market is built to reject; the local-density flywheel is invisible to a playbook that assumes geography is fungible. National tools can copy "AI visibility scoring" — but they cannot copy *scoring the production site they also manage, benchmarked against a Buffalo population, delivered by a named local studio.*

## Redesign implication
The marketing surface stops being a pitch site and becomes a **proof surface**. The homepage's primary action is "Audit your site" — an input box, not a "Book a demo" button; the hero is the scorecard, the benchmark, and the AI-visibility delta, not a value-prop paragraph. The product surface's center of gravity moves from the dashboard to **the receipt** — it must be redesigned as a branded, single-CTA, forwardable artifact (email + shareable web link) with the cross-tenant benchmark line as its emotional payload and an "audit your own site" footer baked in. The audit results page must become a *demo* (store results per-domain, show the "under Strelva" delta, enable the "your score dropped" follow-up drip). Every client-facing output is treated as a distribution surface and designed accordingly: receipts forward, audits convert, benchmarks publish as AEO content. The dark monochrome Business OS dashboard stays, but it is no longer the thing being marketed — the artifacts are.

## Risks & kill signal
- **Risk: the audit pulls tire-kickers, not buyers.** Mitigated by Buffalo targeting (chapter audits surface qualified, present owners) but a real failure mode. **Kill signal:** after embedding the audit and running it at one BNI chapter + publishing 3 benchmark posts, fewer than 1 in 20 audit completions converts to an access-request within 60 days, *and* none of those come from referral/forward.
- **Risk: receipts don't get forwarded** because owners treat them as private utility, not shareable proof. **Kill signal:** instrument the shareable receipt link — if after 8 weeks across all live tenants the forward/share rate is effectively zero, the "artifact-as-distribution" core of the bet is wrong and this collapses back to a normal funnel.
- **Risk: AEO score doesn't move real outcomes** — green audit, no actual AI citations or leads. **Kill signal:** sites Noah ships AEO-ready show no measurable lift in AI-referred sessions (trackable via the existing per-tenant store) within one quarter, which would mean the cold-open is a fear we can't actually resolve.
- **Earliest signal to watch:** does a single owner, unprompted, forward their receipt or run the audit because a peer told them to? That one event proves the loop closes; its absence after the first cohort is the leading indicator the bet is dead.

## Open questions
- What is the actual forward/share rate of a well-designed receipt? The whole bet hinges on it and we have zero data — instrumenting a shareable receipt link is the highest-priority unknown to resolve.
- How many tenants does the benchmark need before "vs. comparable WNY businesses" is statistically honest (not just technically true) per vertical? 6 total may be too thin per-vertical to make the claim defensibly.
- Does the AEO score correlate with real AI citations and real leads, or is it a vanity number? We can validate against the existing per-tenant Search Console / event store.
- Which single vertical do we point the whole flywheel at first — Buffalo wellness (warmest existing tenant overlap) or trades (largest count, sharpest "invisible in AI search" pain)? Density compounds only if we pick one.
- For Jacob's division: does a "workflow teardown" artifact pull the same way an audit does, or does Custom Software's longer sales cycle break the artifact-as-distribution mechanic? Worth a cheap test before assuming the loop transfers.
