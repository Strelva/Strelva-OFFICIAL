# The Storefront Protocol

**One-liner** — Strelva stops being a website vendor and becomes the open standard that connects a local business's site, its data, and the AI agents trying to read and act on it — owning the `/api/v1` contract and capability manifest as a published spec that every tool in the local-business stack reads and writes against, including AI search engines.

## The bet

The consensus play is to be the best *vendor* — the best managed website, the best dashboard, the best automation shop. The non-consensus wager is that in a world where AI agents (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, a client's own Claude/Copilot) are the primary readers and actors on a local business's web presence, the durable position is not the vendor — it's the **protocol the agents read**. Strelva already runs the two hardest pieces of that protocol in production: a versioned `/api/v1` content contract with HMAC-signed revalidation (`src/lib/scaffold-contracts.ts`) and a machine-readable **capability manifest** that declares what a site can do and what actions are allowed (`src/lib/site-capabilities.ts`). If we publish that as an open spec — "the Storefront Protocol" — and make Strelva the reference implementation plus the registry, we stop competing on website quality and start owning the layer every competitor's site eventually has to be expressed in to be legible to AI. The standard-setter captures the relationship even when someone else builds the site.

## Jobs-To-Be-Done served

- **Functional job (owner):** "Make my business legible and actionable to whatever AI a customer or my own staff points at it." Today an owner's site is a wall of HTML that ChatGPT half-reads and no agent can safely act on. The job is *one declared, trustworthy interface* — hours, services, booking, content, and the actions allowed against them — that any agent can read and (under governance) write. The manifest's `allowedActions` (`read | draft | publish | request_custom`) is already that contract.
- **Emotional job:** Remove the dread that "AI is going to describe my business wrong, and I'll never know." The struggling moment from the JTBD research — silent lead decay, a competitor appearing in an AI answer that didn't exist six months ago — is now an *interface* problem, not an SEO problem. The owner wants proof their business is being read correctly by the machines that route customers.
- **Social job:** Look like a real, modern, in-control business — one that "speaks AI" — to customers, staff, and peers. Being a *certified Storefront Protocol site* is a credential, the way "mobile-friendly" was in 2014.
- **Trigger / struggling moments:** (1) An owner asks ChatGPT about their own business and it's wrong or invisible. (2) A customer says "I asked the AI for a plumber and you didn't come up." (3) Jacob's Custom Software clients want their own internal AI/Copilot to pull live business data and can't, because nothing exposes it in a standard, governed way. Each is the moment the *interface* gap becomes visible, not the website gap.

## What Strelva becomes

Strelva becomes the **steward of the Storefront Protocol**: a published spec (the v1 content contract + capability manifest + signed-revalidation handshake, already real in code), a reference implementation (the control plane), a conformance checker (an extension of the audit engine that scores any URL on protocol-readiness), and a registry of conformant sites.

- **Noah / Websites division — the wedge.** Every site Noah ships is *Storefront-Protocol-native by default*: it emits the manifest, exposes the v1 contract, and earns a public conformance score. The pitch shifts from "we manage your website" to "we make your business the canonical, AI-readable source of truth — and we prove it's being read." The free site is still the acquisition wedge; the protocol is what makes the managed relationship sticky and the dashboard's value undeniable (the weekly receipt now includes "AI engines read your site correctly N times this week").
- **Jacob / Custom Software division — the expansion.** Jacob builds workflow automations *as clients against the same protocol*. A trades client's job-scheduling tool, a professional-services intake router — each reads/writes the business's data through the Storefront Protocol contract instead of a one-off integration. The governance engine (`src/lib/ai-governance.ts`: auto / review / block) becomes the protocol's *write-safety layer* — the part no commodity automation shop has. Jacob's division is where the protocol earns its keep: it's the reason a workflow tool built for one trades shop composes onto the next one at near-zero marginal integration cost.
- **Wedge vs. expansion:** Wedge = conformance scoring + protocol-native managed sites (Noah, organic, free). Expansion = protocol-as-integration-substrate for paid workflow software (Jacob) and, later, third-party tools (booking platforms, voice agents, MCP servers) that read/write against the registry.

## Novel Q4-2026 capability exploited

The enabling shift is **MCP crossing from spec to operational standard** (97M+ monthly SDK downloads, 41% of software orgs in production, donated to the Linux Foundation with Anthropic/Google/Microsoft/OpenAI backing) **combined with AEO/GEO becoming the primary local-visibility battleground** (structured data + entity consistency now determines AI-answer citations; 2.5x more AI-Overview appearances with complete schema; 78% of local trades invisible in AI search). Twelve months ago, "publish a protocol for AI to read local-business sites" had no consumer of that protocol — agents couldn't reliably discover or invoke external interfaces. Now there is a *standard transport* (MCP) and a *standard reason* (AI search routes real, higher-converting leads — AI referral traffic converts at ~14% vs ~3% for Google organic). Strelva's move: expose the Storefront Protocol contract as **an MCP server per tenant** — `read_section`, `update_section`, `list_blog_posts` already exist as agent tools in `agent-executor.ts`; wrapping them as MCP turns every Strelva site into something a customer's ChatGPT or a client's internal Copilot can natively discover and act on. The capability manifest *is already* a tool-declaration schema; it was built before there was an ecosystem to consume it. Now there is one.

The flywheel is **conformance as a public, shareable credential**, not a sales pitch — pure pull, zero cold outbound.

- **Buffalo/WNY angle:** Run the conformance check against *every business in one Buffalo vertical corridor* (e.g., Hertel Avenue, Elmwood Village — $150/yr association memberships give dense access to 50+ ICP businesses on the busiest strips) and publish an anonymized **"Western New York AI-Readability Index"**: "62% of Hertel Avenue businesses are invisible to ChatGPT. Here's the one that isn't." This is earned-media bait for Buffalo Rising (1.2M annual readers, local-editorial) and Buffalo Business First — a genuinely novel, locally-specific finding, not a press release. It doubles as AEO content that AI engines themselves cite, compounding the protocol's own visibility.
- **The first 3 concrete distribution acts:**
  1. **Ship the conformance scanner publicly** at `/audit` extended with an "AI Readability / Protocol" category (the audit engine already fetches HTML and runs schema checks; the stubbed `stubGBPCompleteness` / `stubNAPConsistency` / `stubReviewPresence` checks map directly onto protocol-readiness). Any owner scores their own site, sees their gap, and the result page shows "what this looks like as a Storefront Protocol site."
  2. **Publish the WNY AI-Readability Index** for one corridor and bring it to one neighborhood association meeting and one BNI chapter — claim the single "web/AI" seat (one-seat-per-category rule makes this a structural lock). The index is the artifact that gets forwarded.
  3. **Open-source the spec + a `create-storefront-site` starter** (the `custom-repo-starter/` scaffold already exists) and an MCP server template, so any developer — including future Jacob clients and even competitors — builds *against Strelva's contract*. Resend went 0 → 400K users this way: become the default the agents and the docs pick.

## Moat / where value compounds

Value compounds on three axes, each strengthened by every conformant site:

1. **Standard gravity:** Each new site built against the protocol raises the switching cost of the *standard itself*. Tools, MCP servers, and client workflows that read/write the contract make the contract more valuable; the more conformant sites exist, the more any new tool has to support the protocol, and the protocol's reference implementation (Strelva) sits at the center.
2. **Conformance-data moat:** The cross-tenant benchmark layer (already collected, un-mined) becomes *protocol-graded*: "comparable WNY wellness sites score 84 on AI-readability; yours is 51." No single-tenant competitor can produce a population-relative conformance score. This is the latent asset the research flags, made concrete and defensible.
- **Governance moat (Jacob's edge):** As workflow tools write *through* the protocol, the governance engine accumulates a record of which agent writes are safe to auto-apply per vertical. That auto/review/block calibration is proprietary and gets sharper with every governed write — exactly the quality bar commodity vibe-coders and n8n resellers cannot offer.

## Why competitors will not (or cannot) copy this

The consensus playbook treats the website as the *product* and the contract as plumbing. Wix, Duda, GoDaddy, and white-label agencies all ship sites; **none of them publish their internal content API as an open standard**, because their entire business model is lock-in *to their vendor platform* — opening the protocol would commoditize their own moat. Strelva is asymmetric precisely because its site is *free* (acquisition wedge) — it has nothing to protect at the website layer, so it can give away the standard and capture the relationship and data layer instead. A vertical-SaaS incumbent (ServiceTitan) won't go down-market to 1–10 person businesses; a national agency can't credibly publish a *local* AI-readability index for Hertel Avenue. And a 2-person studio cannot be out-run on standard-stewardship by a large vendor whose incentives forbid openness. The one real risk-competitor is a *horizontal* MCP/AEO tooling company — but they have no local managed-site relationship, no cross-tenant local benchmark data, and no governed-write track record, which are the three things that make the standard trustworthy enough for an owner to let an agent write to their site.

## Redesign implication

The marketing surface stops selling "websites" and "dashboards" and starts selling **a standard + a credential**. The hero is the conformance scanner: paste your URL, get your AI-readability score against WNY peers, see "what this looks like as a Storefront Protocol site." The product surface reframes the dashboard around the protocol: the weekly receipt gains an "AI legibility" row (read correctly by N engines, M agent actions handled under governance), the "My Site" view shows the live manifest and conformance score as a thing the owner watches improve, and a public **registry page** lists certified Buffalo businesses (social proof + AEO content in one). The spec itself gets a docs site (clean, quotable, MCP-discoverable — the Resend-style AI-default move). Naming and IA orient around "your business, made readable" rather than "your website, managed."

## Risks & kill signal

- **Risk: nobody consumes the protocol.** A standard with one implementer is just a vendor API. If after 90 days no third party (and no AI engine) is observably reading the manifest/MCP server, the "standard" framing is vanity.
- **Risk: owners don't feel the AI-readability gap.** If conformance scores don't move owner behavior — no one shares the index, no inbound from the scanner — the JTBD was misjudged and we're back to selling websites.
- **Risk: governance liability.** Letting external agents write through the protocol raises the stakes on the auto/review/block engine; one bad auto-applied write erodes the trust the whole standard depends on.
- **Early kill signal (watch in first 6 weeks):** the conformance scanner's share-rate and the AI-Readability Index's forward/earned-media pickup. If the *artifact doesn't get forwarded* in a "City of Good Neighbors" referral market that forwards good proof aggressively, the standard has no pull and the bet is wrong. Conversely, the green-light signal is a Buffalo business asking "how do I get certified" without being sold.
