# The Western New York Index

**One-liner** — Strelva stops selling websites and software and instead builds the only proprietary performance dataset for Western New York local businesses — a "Local-Business Performance Index" — and uses *the act of being measured* as the top-of-funnel, the retention anchor, and the un-copyable moat that every product then attaches to.

## The bet
The non-consensus wager is that **the durable asset Strelva is accidentally building is not the websites and not the software — it is the cross-tenant dataset**, and that a two-person Buffalo studio should treat that dataset as the *flagship product* rather than as exhaust. Every competitor sells an artifact (a site, an automation) and shows the client *their own* numbers; nobody at the local-agency tier can answer the one question every owner actually loses sleep over — "am I doing better or worse than the shop down the street?" — because no competitor runs enough comparable businesses on identical schemas to know. Strelva already does (6+ tenants, one event schema, integrations into Google/Yelp/Instagram/Search Console). The bet is right if "how do I compare to businesses like mine in Buffalo" is a stronger pull than any feature pitch — which JTBD says it is, because the dominant SMB emotion is loss-aversion and the social job is *looking credible relative to peers*, not in the abstract. Build the Index first; the websites and the workflows become the things you *do about* what the Index reveals.

## Jobs-To-Be-Done served
- **Functional job:** "Tell me whether I'm winning or losing against businesses like mine — and what the one change is that would move me." Per-tenant analytics answers "what are my numbers"; only the Index answers "are my numbers *good*." An owner cannot self-serve this — they have exactly one data point (themselves) and no denominator. The Index is the denominator. The functional output is a *rank and a gap*: "Your booking-click rate is 1.8% — the WNY wellness median is 3.1%, and the three studios above you all did the same one thing."
- **Emotional job:** Resolve the ambient dread that you're quietly falling behind without knowing it. A raw number ("47 people found you") soothes; a *ranked* number ("you're in the bottom third of comparable Buffalo trades, here's why") both alarms and relieves — it names the fear precisely and immediately hands over the fix. This is sharper proof-of-life than the receipt alone because it converts a private worry ("is everyone else doing better?") into a measured fact with a next step.
- **Social job:** Look credible *relative to peers*, which is how local owners actually think — not "is my site good" but "is my site as good as the guy who keeps stealing my jobs." A benchmark percentile is the single most shareable credibility artifact a local owner can hold: "we're top-quartile for HVAC web presence in Erie County." They forward that to their BNI chapter unprompted.
- **Trigger / struggling moment:** Not a launch. It's the comparison moment — a competitor appears in a Google AI Overview the owner isn't cited in; a peer at a Chamber event brags about leads; the owner notices their own leads decayed (30/mo → 8/mo) and has no way to know if that's the market or just them. The Index lands *exactly there*: it is the only thing that can tell a worried owner whether their decline is "everyone" or "just you" — and that single distinction is what converts vague anxiety into a signed engagement.

## What Strelva becomes
Strelva becomes **the measurement authority for Western New York local business** — a data company whose two service divisions are the way it monetizes and continuously refreshes the data.

- **The Index is the core asset and the brand.** A proprietary, vertical-segmented, region-scoped dataset of how local businesses actually perform online (web presence, AI-search visibility, review velocity, booking-click rate, search-impression share), continuously fed by every tenant and every audit scan. Public-facing, it is *the Strelva Local-Business Performance Index* — anonymized medians by vertical and region, published. Private-facing, every client sees their own percentile against it.
- **Noah (Websites) plays the wedge and the data-refresh engine.** Each managed site Noah ships is a sensor: it streams the schema-identical event/review/search-console data that keeps the Index live and growing. Noah's monetized dashboard layer stops being "your analytics" and becomes "your rank" — the weekly receipt carries a percentile and a peer-gap line that no single-tenant tool can generate. The free site is still the acquisition wedge, but now its *real* job is to add one more comparable data point to the moat. More clients literally make the product better for every existing client (classic data-network effect, which no local competitor has at this tier).
- **Jacob (Custom Software) plays the expansion and productizes the Index itself.** Two distinct plays: (1) the Index is *built and maintained as software* — the cross-tenant aggregation engine, the percentile computation, the anonymization layer, the public Index site — which is Jacob's first real internal product and a live proof artifact ("we already run a multi-tenant data platform in production"); (2) the Index becomes a *sellable data product to institutions* — a quarterly "State of WNY Local Business Online" benchmark report sold or co-branded with Chambers, BIDs, and trade associations, which doubles as Jacob's distribution channel into the 5-20-person workflow ICP. When the Index reveals an operational pattern ("trades shops in the top quartile all answer inbound within 5 minutes"), that *is* Jacob's discovery-to-workflow lead.
- **Wedge vs expansion:** Wedge = "see how you rank" (the free public Index scan + the percentile in the receipt). Expansion = the monetized dashboard (your live rank + the one move that closes the gap), then Jacob's institutional benchmark report and the workflow builds the Index surfaces as needed.

## Novel Q4-2026 capability exploited
Two newly-possible things converge, neither feasible 12 months ago:

1. **AEO/GEO scoring as a *measurable, comparable* dimension.** The Index's most differentiated axis is "AI-search visibility" — and that only became a real, scoreable metric now. Twelve months ago AI-referred traffic was a rounding error and there was no defensible way to score whether a local business gets *cited in ChatGPT/Perplexity/Google AI Overview answers*. By Q4 2026 the mechanism is known and measurable: JSON-LD completeness + GBP entity consistency + citation presence determine AI-answer appearance, complete structured data earns 2.5x more AI Overview appearances, and 78% of local trades are invisible in AI search. Strelva's audit already parses JSON-LD (`checkSchema` in `src/lib/audit/checks.ts`) and the Phase-2 stubs (`stubGBPCompleteness`, `stubNAPConsistency`, `stubReviewPresence`) map exactly onto AEO factors. Wiring them produces an *AI-Visibility percentile* — "78% of comparable Buffalo trades are invisible in AI search; you're in the visible 22%, ranked #4 in your vertical." No competitor can compute a *regional, vertical-segmented AI-visibility distribution* because none of them measure enough local sites the same way. This is the single most non-consensus number Strelva can own, and the window is open *now* (51% of firms plan to increase AEO investment, only 20% have started).
2. **The dataset only crossed the threshold from anecdote to statistic this year.** A "WNY wellness median booking-click rate" is a hollow claim at 1 client and a defensible statistic at 6+ on identical schemas — which is exactly where Strelva now is. The aggregation, anonymization, and percentile-publishing infrastructure is also newly cheap to build: AI-assisted delivery gives a 2-person team a 68% speed advantage, so Jacob can stand up the cross-tenant aggregation engine and public Index site in days, not a quarter — work that previously needed a data team.

## Organic GTM motion
The flywheel: **Public Index pulls owners in ("where do I rank?") → free audit scan adds them as a measured data point → free managed site makes them a live sensor → monetized "your rank" dashboard retains them → their shareable percentile recruits peers → each new business sharpens the Index for everyone.** Every loop *increases the value of the asset itself*, which is what separates this from a normal lead funnel.

**Buffalo/WNY angle:** the Index is *only credible because it is local and dense*. "How do you compare to businesses like yours" is meaningless nationally and devastating locally — Buffalo's concentration of trades (1,417 Erie County construction/trades establishments, 35-year employment high) and tight referral graph means a vertical-and-region-segmented benchmark hits home in a way a national tool never could. The "City of Good Neighbors" norm rewards an embedded local authority and penalizes generic remote vendors; *being the studio that publishes the WNY benchmark* is a positioning no out-of-town competitor can claim. Institutions (Buffalo Niagara Partnership, BNI chapters, Hertel/Elmwood associations, Chambers) all want exactly this data about their members and have no way to produce it — making the Index a co-marketing payload, not a cold pitch.

**First 3 concrete distribution acts (no cold outbound):**
1. **Publish a single anonymized benchmark stat as earned media.** Compute one defensible number from the existing 6 tenants + a batch of audit scans across one vertical ("Median Buffalo wellness studio scores 58/100 on AI-search visibility; the top quartile scores 80+") and pitch it to Buffalo Rising / Buffalo Business First as a local-data story. It is genuinely novel, locally relevant, earned (not paid), and it makes Strelva *the source*.
2. **Bring the Index to one BNI chapter or the BNP free tier as a "where does your category rank" live read.** Walk in with the segmented benchmark for the trades/wellness categories in the room. Take the open "web/marketing" seat. The data is the entire reason they let you present — and the percentile each owner gets is the artifact they forward.
3. **Stand up the public Index scan page** (`/index` or extend `/audit`): any owner enters their URL, gets their AI-visibility + web-presence percentile against the WNY vertical distribution, gated by email. This converts the existing audit engine from a one-shot scorecard into a *ranking* — a strictly stronger pull — and stores results by domain so a "your rank dropped" follow-up becomes a zero-cost drip.

## Moat / where value compounds
Every new client and every audit scan adds a data point, which **tightens every percentile and widens the gap a competitor would have to close to produce comparable benchmarks.** This is a data-network effect at the local-vertical tier, where it has never existed: a rival agency would need to (a) sign a comparable number of *the same kind* of WNY businesses, (b) instrument them on identical schemas, (c) run them long enough to have trend data, and (d) do all of it *before* Strelva's lead compounds — a multi-year cold start against a moving target. The Index also compounds *trust*: the more Strelva is cited as "the source" for WNY local-business benchmarks (earned media, institutional reports), the more it becomes the default answer, and the harder it is to displace a measurement authority. And it compounds *across both divisions* — Jacob's workflow leads come from patterns only the Index can see, so the data asset feeds the software pipeline that the software pipeline feeds.

## Why competitors will not (or cannot) copy this
The consensus playbook treats client data as *exhaust* and sells *per-tenant analytics* — "here are your numbers" — because that is all a single-tenant tool or a fresh agency can produce. National platforms (Duda, Wix, GoDaddy, Semrush, Birdeye) *have* scale but not *local density per vertical*: their benchmarks are national averages that mean nothing to a Buffalo HVAC shop comparing against Buffalo HVAC shops. Local agencies have the locality but not the multi-tenant instrumentation — they hand-build sites without a shared schema, so they hold zero comparable data. The asymmetry is structural: **you need both local vertical density and identical multi-tenant instrumentation, and the only way to get the data is to already be doing the thing Strelva is uniquely doing** (running multiple WNY local sites on one control-plane schema). A competitor who decides to copy this in Q4 2026 starts from one data point and a multi-year accumulation curve, while Strelva is already publishing. The consensus misses it because everyone is optimizing the *artifact* (faster site generation, more features) and treating the dashboard as a retention feature — nobody is treating the *cross-customer intelligence* as the flagship product, which is precisely where the hardest-to-copy vertical-SaaS moats actually live.

## Redesign implication
The marketing surface stops leading with "we build managed websites" and leads with **the Index as the hero**: a live, public, beautifully-rendered "Western New York Local-Business Performance Index" — medians and distributions by vertical, the headline AI-visibility gap, a "scan your site to see your rank" primary CTA. The homepage *is* a data product. The product surface re-centers the dashboard on **rank, not raw numbers**: the Overview becomes "your percentile vs comparable WNY businesses" with the peer-gap and the one closing move foregrounded; the weekly receipt's hero line becomes the benchmark comparison ("you moved from bottom-third to median this month"). Both divisions get a shared proof spine — "the studio that runs the WNY Index" — that earns Jacob's software credibility before a workflow ships. The audit page is reframed from "score" to "rank," and a new institutional surface (a downloadable/co-brandable quarterly benchmark report) becomes the Chamber/association distribution artifact.

## Risks & kill signal
- **Statistical thinness risk (primary):** at 6 tenants, per-vertical-per-region cells may be too small to publish a *defensible* median without it being noise. Kill signal: if, after the first vertical batch (managed tenants + audit scans), no segment reaches a sample size you'd stand behind publicly, the Index can't yet be the hero and this collapses back into the receipt-benchmark line of the proof-engine approach. Mitigation: bootstrap density with audit-scan data (you can scan hundreds of public WNY sites without signing them) so the *distribution* is real even when the *managed* count is small.
- **"So what" risk:** owners might find the rank interesting but not act — a benchmark that doesn't convert to a signed engagement is a vanity metric. Early signal to watch: do owners who see a *bad* percentile in the public scan convert to access-requests at a higher rate than the flat audit did? If the rank doesn't lift conversion over the existing audit, the framing isn't load-bearing.
- **Anonymization/trust risk:** publishing benchmarks while managing the underlying clients requires airtight anonymization, or it erodes the exact local trust the moat depends on. Kill signal: any client objection to being part of an aggregate. Mitigation: opt-in framing ("your data helps build the benchmark you get to see"), aggregates only, never per-business disclosure.
