# Strelva — Jobs-To-Be-Done Map

> The job is what the customer is trying to get done — not the artifact they buy. This
> map grounds the 12 divergent approaches in two real buyers and the moments that make
> them act. It also maps each approach to the primary job(s) it serves and flags the
> jobs the consensus market mis-serves. Source evidence: `research/_DIGEST.md` §5.

There are two customer sets and one shared meta-job.

- **Customer A — the SMB owner, re: web presence** (Division 1 / Noah). 1–10 person local
  business: wellness, food, restaurant, trades, professional, salon, retail.
- **Customer B — the SMB operator, re: software / workflows** (Division 2 / Jacob).
  ~5–20 person business with repetitive processes, tool sprawl, a spreadsheet ceiling.
  Trades + professional services are highest-fit early.
- **Shared meta-job:** *"Make my business less dependent on my personal attention — and
  prove to me it's handled."*

---

## Customer A — Web presence (Division 1)

**Core functional job.** "Get me customers without me having to think about the website."
The real hire is **abdication of the web problem**, not ownership of a tool. 98.7% of SMB
owners expect revenue from their site, yet behave set-and-forget. They want it resolved,
not managed.

**Emotional job.** Remove the ambient anxiety that the website is silently failing. Owners
don't open GA4 ("nobody looks at it"). The weekly "47 people found you, 6 called" is a
**receipt — proof of life**, not analytics.

**Social job.** Look credible — real, established, still-in-business — to a customer before
first contact. For trades and professional services the site is identity, not utility.

**Trigger / struggling moments** (loss aversion + embarrassment, never aspiration):
- Lead volume quietly drops (30/mo in 2022 → 8 in 2025) and the owner can't say why.
- A customer mentions the site looks broken on mobile.
- A competitor appears in Google / an AI answer that wasn't there six months ago.

**What they currently hire — and why it fires:**
| Hire | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| One-time agency build | Agency goes dark; site rots; forms break silently |
| DIY (Squarespace / Wix) | They can't maintain it; tech anxiety wins |
| Nothing (27% have no site) | Non-consumption: perceived irrelevance + tech intimidation |

**Dominant blocking force:** anxiety — "what if the AI changes something wrong?" Not lack
of desire. The real competitor is **inertia**, not a named rival.

**Non-obvious mis-served job:** Every competitor sells the ARTIFACT (the website). The
unmet job is ongoing **EVIDENCE** the artifact is performing — the "is it working?" loop
closed weekly, in plain language, automatically. **The receipt is the product; the website
is the delivery vehicle.**

## Customer B — Software / workflows (Division 2)

**Core functional job.** "Make my business run without me being the bottleneck." The hire
is **operational autonomy** — the ability to leave for a week without the business seizing
up. 80% of SMBs cite founder-dependency as a critical operational failure; 95% of
secondary pain is undocumented process living in one person's head and inbox.

**Emotional job.** Feel like a real business, not a chaos manager. Kill the low-grade dread
that something is falling through the cracks.

**Social job (dual).** Appear competent and in control to staff and clients; build
something with value beyond the founder — exit-ready, not a personality cult. Paying
$500–$2,000/month for disconnected SaaS nobody fully uses produces waste-shame.

**Trigger / struggling moments** ("I almost lost a client / almost missed payroll", never
"I want to modernize"):
- A critical employee leaves and takes tribal knowledge.
- A job slips because an estimate never became an invoice / a lead never got entered.
- The spreadsheet that "runs" a process breaks when a new hire touches it.
- A third of a meeting is spent hunting for info that should be one click away.

**What they currently hire — and why it fires:**
| Hire | Fires when |
| --- | --- |
| Off-the-shelf SaaS (ClickUp/Notion/Monday) | Handles 80%; the 20% where advantage lives is always custom |
| Spreadsheets | The business outgrows them; data-integrity wall |
| Fractional ops consultants | Too slow, too abstract, no code output |

**Dominant blocking force:** anxiety — "I don't know what I'm asking for" + "the last dev
shop over-promised." Not lack of desire. Again, the real competitor is **inertia /
undocumented tribal knowledge**, not other software shops.

**Non-obvious mis-served job:** Discovery shops sell "custom software." The unmet job is
**documented operational certainty** — a business a stranger could walk in and run by
following the system. The product isn't an app; it's organizational resilience. The owner
is buying peace of mind and personal freedom; competitors pitch technology.

## The shared meta-job and the cross-division leverage

Both buyers are the *same person* at two struggling moments three months apart. The owner
who triggers on the front door (leads dropped; site looks broken) is the owner who triggers
on the back office (employee left; estimate fell through a crack). Today those moments go to
two vendors. The strategic leverage: the studio already inside the front door at moment one
is the obvious, trusted answer at moment two — **no second vendor-trust hurdle.** Strelva's
audit engine, benchmark data, governance engine, and weekly receipt are latent proof assets
that earn Division 2 credibility *before a single workflow ships*.

---

## The four non-obvious jobs the consensus mis-serves

These are where the asymmetry lives. Every strong approach exploits at least one.

1. **"Prove it's working" (not "build the artifact").** The market ships websites; nobody
   closes the weekly evidence loop in plain language. → *the receipt.*
2. **"Tell me if my numbers are good" (not "show me my numbers").** Per-tenant analytics
   has no denominator. Only a multi-tenant platform can answer "good vs. comparable
   businesses like me in Buffalo." → *the cross-tenant benchmark.*
3. **"Make me legible to the AI that customers now ask first" (not "rank me on Google").**
   78% of local trades are invisible in AI search; the discovery surface moved and the
   owner can't self-diagnose it. → *AI-answer readiness.*
4. **"Reduce my anxiety so I can buy" (not "give me more features").** For both buyers the
   binding constraint is comprehension and trust, not price. The governed guardrail (a
   named human signs off) and teaching-before-selling directly dissolve this. → *governance
   + proof, not features.*

**Reading the map.** The cluster around **job 2 (benchmark)** is the most crowded —
approaches 05, 09, 12, and partly 01 all bet the cross-tenant local benchmark is the
durable moat. The cluster around **job 3 (AI legibility)** is approaches 07, 08, and partly
01/04/12. **Job 1 (the receipt)** is the connective tissue under almost every approach — it
is the lowest-cost, highest-certainty win and shows up as a supporting mechanic in 01, 03,
04, 05, 09, 10, 11. **Job 4 (anxiety)** is the explicit thesis of 02, 03, and 10. This
clustering is itself a finding: the synthesis treats the receipt + the benchmark + AI
legibility as the three through-lines because nearly every strong approach reaches for the
same small set of latent assets. See `SYNTHESIS.md`.
