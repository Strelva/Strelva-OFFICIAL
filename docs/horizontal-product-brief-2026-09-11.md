# Strelva horizontal product brief

Status: direction confirmed by Jacob on September 11 and clarified through September 20,
2026. Local implementation of the topology below is authorized. This records
product intent, not implemented capability or production release approval.

## September 20 website release focus

Jacob selected websites as the main current commercial focus, with self-service
as the primary experience. The intended customer promise is to create a useful
business website and keep improving it as the business grows. Agencies use the
same product for separately owned client work; Strelva or an agency may provide
help when requested. Human delivery must be identified wherever the current
implementation still requires it.

The release review now follows the website journey: first visit, account entry,
creation or supported connection, preview, publication, customer activity, later
changes and recovery. Intake, bookings, documents and Checks support that journey
where their connections are implemented and verified. Broader workspace and app
capabilities remain part of the product, with their existing acceptance and
compatibility obligations retained. They do not define the main launch promise.

Three entry paths need separate evidence: an existing Strelva-managed website,
a new website for a new customer, and a website hosted outside Strelva. An audit
of a public URL does not establish ownership, editing access or an import path.
The established managed-client implementation does not by itself prove public
self-service creation. New website creation, outside-site connection and each
publishing path must be described according to their actual supported behavior.

Candidate customer segments span independent professionals, small businesses,
growing teams, larger departments and agencies. The initial evaluation emphasis
is small businesses, growing teams and small web/digital agencies. This is a
selected research focus, not evidence of demand or enterprise readiness. New
self-service prices, included quantities and the free-to-paid boundary remain
open; subscriptions with included usage and a spending cap remain the selected
paid model. Existing managed website prices and accepted customer agreements
remain unchanged.

The [acceptance ledger](./strelvav2-horizontal-acceptance.md#website-release-review)
owns findings and proof. Local preparation remains authorized; production
activation and external actions retain their existing approval boundaries.

### Self-service website implementation

After the website review, Jacob requested implementation of the missing customer
journey. A person describes their business, receives a real saved website draft,
previews and edits that same artifact, approves its exact revision and prepares
it for launch. Returning to the workspace reopens the saved site. An agency is
optional; an operator build request is not a substitute for this path.

Private drafts can start in the person's own workspace. Published client sites
retain their own repository and deployment. The control plane owns the brief,
version history, customer authority and launch evidence; reusable client rendering
belongs in the starter before use in generated client repositories. A preview
must come from the saved artifact that export and launch use, not a separate
fictional example. Edits invalidate approval of an older candidate. Failed or
unconfigured providers must leave a visible, recoverable state without claiming
that a website went live.

The same implementation wave finishes explicit agency draft preparation for
existing managed sites and connects versioned inquiries and native bookings to
representative client pages. It preserves native tenant publication and provider
approval boundaries. Public URL checks still do not establish ownership or an
import capability. New commercial amounts are not selected by this instruction;
existing customer terms and the production activation boundary remain unchanged.

## Product

The mission is to make ambition affordable: give businesses access to an
exceptional team while preserving people's judgment, participation and
relationships. Managed Websites is the established operating base. Strong
vertical offerings use shared capabilities; agencies can use Strelva's offerings,
operate them for clients, and help develop new ones.

Strelva helps people discover useful improvements, build products, and get ongoing
work done. It is horizontal across small businesses and large organizations.
Agencies are users, not the primary audience that defines the entire product.
People and authorized agents can use the same underlying abilities.

The existing agency approach is the operating starting point. Self-service,
Strelva-managed delivery, and outside-agency participation are fluid ways of
working together. They are not permanent customer categories. A person can ask
for advice, request review, delegate a task, or assign ongoing responsibility
without moving the work or losing context and history.

People should receive value without knowing what to ask for, replacing their
tools, or completing an organization setup first. Relevant examples, templates,
demonstrations, conversations, and permitted business context help them begin.
Organization setup and connections appear when the work requires them.

## September 16 transition and hypotheses

Jacob confirmed this account of the transition on September 16. Strelva is moving
from a relationship centered on hiring us to deliver and maintain something
specific toward a product businesses can use to improve and operate their work,
with Strelva and other providers participating when needed. The central hypothesis
is that capability customers previously accessed by hiring us can become something
they access through a product. Human help remains a useful, explicitly agreed
part of the offering.

Improving work customers already do and enabling worthwhile work they previously
could not justify are equally part of this direction. Existing work includes
manual processes, work performed by employees or outside providers, and work
already supported by software. Value may come from less effort, fewer errors,
faster completion, better service, or easier adaptation as the business changes.
Replacement of an existing tool or provider is an offering-specific choice;
improvements can also work within and across the systems a customer keeps.

For example, a business might bring its existing staff request process, including
its spreadsheet, messages, approvals, and follow-up. Strelva could help improve
that process, create only the missing software, and support later changes. This
is an illustrative experience, not evidence of customer adoption or live support.

The following hypotheses explain what must become true for the transition to
succeed. Their IDs are stable references for future evidence. Selected direction
does not establish that these customer behaviors or economics have been proven.

| ID | Starting point | Intended change and hypothesis | Evidence that would support it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TH-01 | Customers hire us for a website or defined project | Trust earned through one useful result can extend into a recurring product relationship across several business needs | Customers voluntarily return for a second need and continue using the product |
| TH-02 | We identify opportunities, scope them, and deliver them | Enough of our expertise can become usable product behavior that customers discover improvements, begin work, and obtain value without our involvement every time | Independent users reach useful results and recover from ordinary errors with measured, manageable assistance |
| TH-03 | A customer brings us a recognizable purchase | An existing process, spreadsheet, document, or loosely stated problem can become a useful improvement without lengthy consulting | Real work improves against its current baseline after counting setup, switching, learning, review, and correction |
| TH-04 | We deliver and maintain a particular digital asset | Customers will authorize us to improve and operate related business work in existing systems when benefit and control are clear | Customers grant bounded access, retain recurring work after real exceptions, and can inspect results and revoke access |
| TH-05 | Changes return to us as requests | Customers and authorized collaborators can propose, inspect, and release useful changes while their applications and records remain reliable | Customers make meaningful changes, continue using the result, and recover from failures without a new bespoke engagement each time |
| TH-06 | We are the primary builder and service provider | Owners, employees, agencies, and agents can participate around the same business with less handoff effort | Real handoffs preserve records and responsibility, reduce repeated explanation, and allow customer control to resume |
| TH-07 | Delivery knowledge lives with us and in individual implementations | Solutions can become reusable offerings across businesses, supported by shared capabilities and specific domain knowledge | Later installations take less setup and support; updates preserve customer differences; reuse works across distinct offerings |
| TH-08 | Revenue follows a defined service agreement | Customers will pay subscriptions and usage for recurring product value, and revenue can grow faster than human delivery effort | Paid continuation and improving margins after counting setup, review, correction, provider charges, retries, maintenance, and support |
| TH-09 | We learn mainly through projects we sell | Internal research and observed product use can systematically identify valuable opportunities and improve repeatable offerings | Findings lead to tested improvements, repeated customer outcomes, and lower intervention costs without exposing private customer information |

The main risk is a broader interface with roughly the same bespoke delivery
effort behind it. Independent customer value, repeat use, and falling human effort
per comparable result would support the transition. Continuing founder dependence,
expensive customization, weak paid continuation, or maintenance that grows with
every installation would count against it. A completed implementation alone
cannot settle these hypotheses.

For existing work, record how the customer accomplishes it today, who participates,
what it costs, what works well, and what changes with Strelva. Preserve useful
judgment, reassurance, and relationships. Compare against the current process,
reasonable improvements to existing tools, and a capable general assistant where
relevant. Measure customer time, cash costs, quality, and revenue effects
separately; time released is not automatically cash saved. For previously undone
work, look for actual use and willingness to continue after the first result.

Customer ownership, permissions, business data separation, existing agreements,
and explicit acceptance of service remain commitments. They do not depend on
whether these growth hypotheses succeed. First offerings, prices, numerical
success thresholds, and release choices remain open as recorded below.

The [evidence register](./product-reality.md#september-16-transition-hypotheses)
records the current proof limits. This section owns the transition account;
research alternatives and local implementation records do not establish market
validation or change production authority.

## September 18 practical scalability clarification

Jacob defines practical scalability as less work and friction for Strelva while

## Agreed capabilities and constraints

### September 15 product topology

The business is the enduring home. An offering states a concrete promise and
can combine software, completed work and ongoing service. An installation is
that offering configured for a particular business, with its version, connected
resources, access and explicitly accepted responsibility. An offering is not an
executable capability, and installing it does not itself authorize external work.
Ordinary documents and one-off work remain useful without an installation.

Five customer actions carry across offerings: use business information, make
something usable, put it to work, change how it works, and work with people or
external AI. Owners use the delivered thing with changes and help beside it.
Employees use their permitted applications. Agencies see their client portfolio
and then enter separate client work. Strelva staff handle work they have accepted.
Customer portals and branded addresses are entrances to the same business, not
new ownership or permission systems. An address never grants access.

An assignment identifies who may operate specified work, for what purpose,
within which limits and until when. Owner, payer, provider, approver and acting
person remain separate. A customer can request a provider's help but cannot
declare that the provider has accepted a service commitment. An agency leaving
ends its access; customer records and agreed deliverables remain. Agency methods
and other clients' information remain private.

Configuration may be inferred from permitted context and adjusted immediately.
Spending, publishing, contacting people, sharing information and destructive
actions still require explicit authority. Changes use risk-appropriate checks,
sample cases and visible consequences. Recovery preserves later records and
discloses external effects that cannot be reversed.

Shared module candidates are business context and records; offering setup and
delivery; execution and ongoing responsibility; changes and recovery;
participation and handoff; and usage and budgets. Share safety obligations now
and other behavior when actual offerings demonstrate reuse. Do not replace
product-native permissions, release checks or recovery with generic promises.

This direction supersedes the September 14 restriction on opening new horizontal
implementation work. The staff application remains a regression and acceptance
journey, not a limit on the product. Local work must connect usable offerings,
not merely add schemas or preview screens. Production and external actions remain
separately authorized.

### September 14 architecture clarification

Jacob specified one shared system for owners, employees, agencies, Strelva staff,
and external users. Someone
brings a business need; Strelva can deliver the appropriate software, document,
analysis, external action or ongoing service. Other people use the finished
result without learning its creation process. Stronger models expand qualified
capability without silently changing authority or released applications.

The owner opens a business, finds its work, and uses the live application,
document or website. Proposed changes appear with that same item. Review shows
exactly what will change before publication; history and recovery stay there.
The interaction is: business → work → live thing → proposed change → review →
live thing. Drafts, deployments, jobs and executions are implementation details,
not separate destinations the owner must navigate to finish this loop.

The business navigation is Home, Work, Ongoing, People & access, and Settings,
with New, Search, Explore offerings, and Help available as utilities. Personal
Account is separate from business Settings. Applications, documents and websites
are types of work, not required permanent navigation categories. The local
shell connects these destinations to existing work and permission controls;
this does not establish that every future offering is implemented or released.
Staff and customers open only the application, document, form or website they
need. Strelva's team maintains delivery and handles failures through its internal
controls. Agencies have explicit access to independently owned businesses.
Ownership, payer, creator, approver and operator remain distinct.

The September 14 acceptance journey is one complete staff request application:
ask, create, understand, use, share, receive employee submissions, propose a
change, inspect its exact effect, publish, recover from a failure and continue.
The live application remains available throughout, and records survive publishing
and recovery. Preserve this experience while extending the September 15 topology.
Its recorded proof does not establish that later additions work.

Native composable interfaces, custom deployed applications and work in existing
systems are all required architectural paths. Fixed parts remain the maintained
native starting point; they are not a permanent ceiling on supported behavior.
Custom code requires isolated construction and a tested release, permissions,
data migration, maintenance and recovery contract. Application definitions and
customer records have separate lifecycles; released applications remain usable
while candidate changes are prepared.

The [source audit and build order](./horizontal-audit-and-plan-2026-09-11.md)
records current gaps and connected acceptance journeys. This clarification
supersedes screen-first sequencing and any interpretation that bounded native
apps alone complete the horizontal target. It selects no infrastructure vendor,
commercial terms or production action.

### Existing product constraints

- Build through proven parts, custom behavior, custom applications, existing
  tools, and bounded third-party agent work. Distinguish discussion, experiments,
  and reliably supported operation. Fixed interface components remain the
  starting design rule; custom behavior does not imply arbitrary visual output.
- Templates can be starting designs, working products, or ongoing services.
  State which is being offered and adapt to business context.
- Maintain sourced facts, preferences, procedures, and results. Learned
  preferences never confer permission.
- Expand autonomy through explicit limits and evidence. Publishing, spending,
  deletion, permission changes, and outbound messages require an explicit action
  or applicable standing rule. Sharing and delegation do not expand authority.
- Support parallel contributions to separate parts, conflict detection, scoped
  review, and concise handoff briefs with permitted underlying history.
- Agencies reuse products and delivery methods. Shared updates preserve client
  choices and undergo client-specific checks. Agency methods stay private by
  default; customer data and credentials do not become reusable templates.
- Internal delivery uses the same product abilities offered to outside agencies,
  except platform administration and internal R&D. Agency client relationships
  and any Strelva delivery partnership must be explicit.
- Clients own their business data. Each offering states portability, maintenance,
  and source-code terms; universal source-code ownership is not selected.
- Self-service readiness requires independent users to obtain value, make
  changes, understand costs and permissions, and recover from ordinary errors.

## Economics

Selected pricing direction: generous subscriptions with useful included work,
understandable usage and a customer spending limit. Higher subscriptions can
include explicitly bounded human help. Credits may reconcile capabilities but
must not obscure what customers consume. Shared organization or agency billing
and business-funded budgets can coexist, with one explicit payer per job.
Collaboration is initially included. Strelva absorbs retries caused by its own
failures. Exact prices, allowances, metering and any results-based pricing remain
open. Local budget accounting is not proof of subscription billing.

On September 18, Jacob selected the payer-change rule: a business owner proposes
a new payer, and that exact person must accept. Only jobs created after that
acceptance use the new payer. Existing jobs, reservations and unresolved costs
remain with the original payer and accepted limits. The acceptance transaction
sets the effective boundary; a concurrent job must belong to one side of it.
This does not transfer an old debt, change a subscription or authorize a charge.

Agency co-creation can earn explicitly awarded subscription or usage credits,
and later royalties tied to a particular offering and agreement. This is not
company equity, automatic ownership, a referral entitlement or a reward for an
unaccepted idea. Contribution evidence, awards and commercial acceptance remain
separate from customer data access. No royalty rate or payout is selected.

Measure time and financial effects separately, including setup, human review,
correction, support, model/tool charges, and maintenance. Do not infer saved labor
or increased revenue from task completion alone. Existing agreements and billing
remain unchanged until a separate commercial and release decision.

## Vertical R&D

The [definition of done](./strelvav2-definition-of-done.md) expands this direction
into a proposed evidence-to-outcome learning loop: recurring research, problem
discovery, user understanding, frontier capability assessment, divergent
possibilities, strategy, product coherence, goal-based experience checks,
construction/verification, and postlaunch learning. These are internal work
requirements, not ten customer navigation sections or implemented services.

The foundation stays horizontal. Internal R&D investigates verticals deeply to
produce business knowledge, reusable offerings, and evidence of customer value.
It includes a safe testing workspace, product experiments, and budgeted
agent-assisted research. Experiments do not become supported capabilities merely
because a demonstration works.

Select research using customer access, measurable economic opportunity, reuse,
and learning transferable to other contexts. Keep industry-specific behavior
specific until actual use establishes what should be shared. Multiple customer
results and support-inclusive economics are needed before a vertical is called
proven. Industry packs are optional contextual entry points, not restrictions.
