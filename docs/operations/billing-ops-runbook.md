# Agency billing operations

Prepared 2026-10-07 for [#420](https://github.com/Strelva/Strelva-OFFICIAL/issues/420), baseline `115448a9`. **Manual operating procedure around inspected code, not proof of live billing readiness.** `[JACOB]` means approval or financial/production action. **After #283** means Connect-dependent work; #283 alone is insufficient without its dependent ledger, refund/dispute, payer and payout flows. This task authorizes documentation, push and PR only; no live Stripe calls or changes.

Jacob owns Strelva's financial decisions. Agents trace authorized evidence, reconcile amounts and prepare proposed actions and responses. They do not press Refund, submit dispute evidence, change cards/subscriptions, move funds or promise credits. The actual agency/business merchant owns its retail sale; Strelva's wholesale subscription and an agency's retail subscription are separate obligations. Strelva's own agency gets the same platform terms and controls.

## Case record and daily reconciliation

At the staffed checks in the [support model](./support-model.md), review operator alerts/queue gaps plus the authorized billing inbox and Stripe dispute/failed-payment deadlines. Code does not put every financial event in the queue. `[JACOB]` choose financial-access roles, backup, actual inbox, record storage and notification settings before operation. Sensitive records stay outside this public repo; keep provider references rather than card data, credentials or raw identity documents.

1. Verify requester, business/agency ID, beneficiary, actual payer and merchant separately. Do not infer payer from agency-of-record, account email or client-service responsibility. Verify agreement and authority to ask for the proposed financial action.
2. Open private case `BILL-[date]-[id]`: owner, impact, first receipt, Stripe environment/account context, `cus_`, `sub_`, `in_`, `pi_`/`ch_`, event/refund/dispute IDs where applicable; amount/currency; prior refunds/credits; agreement version; next deadline and checkpoint. Record only IDs actually present. Include actions already accepted and uncertainty.
3. Compare provider state with tenant subscription ID/status/plan/past-due timestamp, account bundle and released business-billing mirror. Record mismatches. A cached status or admin panel amount cannot prove an invoice was paid. No blind SQL patch or forced `active` state.
4. Prepare `[JACOB]` action: exact merchant/account/object, amount/currency, reason/terms, effect on future billing/service, duplicate/reversal risk and verification plan. Obtain approval before live mutation; a general support policy is not authority for every case.
5. Read back provider outcome, retain action/reference and approval, reconcile application state through its supported path, and send only the approved update. Record failed local audit/mirror/notification independently. Close after verified outcome; pending refund or unresolved dispute retains an owner and next checkpoint.

Proposed `[JACOB]` first response: blocked payment within one staffed business day; ordinary refund/service dispute within two; card dispute reviewed at next staffed intake and escalated immediately when due within two business days. Stripe's actual response deadline takes precedence. No bank refund-arrival or dispute-win guarantee.

## Refunds on Strelva charges — manual today

1. Locate the original payment in the correct Stripe environment/account; inspect successful/captured amount, currency, cumulative refunds, dispute state and any credit note. Determine whether this is a recurring subscription, one-off pay link or another charge. Confirm the real seller and applicable terms.
2. `[JACOB]` decide eligibility and exact amount/reason; choose whether future service/subscription changes separately. No refund policy, automatic goodwill allowance or amount threshold is accepted by this document. If disputed, do not send another credit blindly; use the dispute procedure below.
3. `[JACOB]` in Stripe Dashboard, refund the selected original payment for the approved amount. Refund to the original payment method; do not collect a replacement card by email or create an unrelated payout. Check cumulative refunds cannot exceed the original amount. Do not repeat a timed-out click until the payment's refund list is inspected.
4. Save `re_` ID, status and related balance evidence. Pending is not refunded; failed/canceled requires investigation and a newly approved remedy. Original processing fees are generally not returned; insufficient available balance can delay a card refund or fail other refund types. See [Stripe refunds](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds) (checked 2026-10-07).
5. Reconcile case/accounting manually: this webhook does not consume `charge.refunded`/refund events, so HTTP 200 cannot prove REB recorded a refund. Do not decrement an internal work allowance or cancel a subscription as an invented refund-sync mechanism. Any invoice credit/tax adjustment needs its actual supported accounting treatment and `[JACOB]` approval. Communicate approved refund status and provider reference, without claiming the bank has posted it.

**After #283 — connected payments / shares:** additionally identify direct, destination or separate-charge/transfer context. A direct-charge refund debits its connected merchant; a destination/separate refund debits the platform, and recovering recipient transfers is a separate operation. For separate charges/transfers, refunding does not automatically reverse transfers. `[JACOB]` approve each reversal and any application-fee refund under the chosen agreement; reconcile negative split rows, transfer reversal IDs, recipient balance and shortfall. Do not activate until the relevant Connect ledger/reversal implementation is tested. Sources: [refund charge contexts](https://docs.stripe.com/refunds#refunds-through-a-connect-platform), [separate charges/transfers](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/separate-charges-and-transfers).

## Card disputes and service disagreements

**Manual today for Strelva charges:**

1. Read the Stripe dispute, reason, exact deadline, amount/fees, charge/account and evidence already submitted. Capture `dp_` ID and deadline in UTC with an earlier human checkpoint. App webhook processing does not ingest `charge.dispute.*`; ensure a real human sees Stripe's notice. If the deadline is imminent, interrupt ordinary review work.
2. Distinguish cardholder chargeback from a support disagreement. For the latter, verify actual scope and delivery receipts, seek a proposed resolution and route any refund through the prior section. A complaint does not by itself authorize canceling grants, deleting data or changing the payer.
3. Prepare a concise evidence packet: accepted terms, authorized payer, invoice, delivered result/version, use/approval/receipt evidence, communications and any prior refund. Check dates/amounts against provider records. Agents may draft; Jacob validates accuracy and privacy. Do not fabricate delivery or consent from a generated artifact.
4. `[JACOB]` choose accept or contest based on evidence and recoverable amount versus time/fees. Stripe normally debits the disputed amount and fees pending outcome. Submitting evidence is consequential and generally final; assemble complete evidence before submission. Follow the actual Stripe deadline, not an assumed universal number of days. [Stripe responding](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/responding) (checked 2026-10-07).
5. `[JACOB]` submit once in the actual charge's Dashboard context. Preserve submitted evidence, time and confirmation. An open dispute is not resolved because a support reply was sent. A customer withdrawing a dispute does not replace required evidence handling. Avoid duplicate reimbursement while a dispute/refund is open.
6. Track won/lost/closed outcome and balance transactions, reconcile case/accounting, notify only authorized parties and record labor/losses. A lost dispute is not reversed by an app rollback. `[JACOB]` decide service continuation separately under accepted terms and public-site preservation requirements.

**After #283 — Connect disputes:** locate the actual charging account and charge type before allocating responsibility. Direct charges debit the connected account; destination/separate charges debit the platform. The selected connected-merchant loss collector determines unrecoverable negative-balance responsibility; it does not eliminate Strelva's risk on Strelva's own charges. `on_behalf_of` is not a liability transfer. Recipient share/royalty agreements and reversals need the implemented split ledger plus explicit approval; no automatic recovery or reserve is assumed. [Stripe Connect disputes](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/disputes), [risk management](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/risk-management).

Workspace research `docs/research/strelva-agency-money-and-verification-2026-10-07.md`, §§1–6, explains wholesale versus retail, Accounts v2 configurations, irreversible fee/loss-collector choices, transfer versus bank payout, and Jacob's loss-liability decision. **After #283** verify actual enabled configuration, platform approval and event account routing; research is not live setup evidence. `[JACOB]` accepts loss liability, reserve/shortfall handling, fee collection, agreement clawbacks and recovery authority. An agency merchant being verified does not approve the business's merchant or a Strelva platform charge.

## Failed payments and dunning

1. Inspect the failed invoice and current Stripe subscription/payment attempt. [Webhook](../../src/app/api/billing/webhook/route.ts) resolves metadata, then subscription/customer mappings; sets `past_due` while preserving the first failure time; alerts operator and attempts dunning. If no tenant is found, it can acknowledge without updating the tenant: treat as mapping/reconciliation failure.
2. Check actual notification outcome. [sendPaymentPastDueEmail](../../src/lib/delivery-email.ts) uses `audience: "client"` despite the webhook comment suggesting a billing/operator bypass; client mail needs `EMAIL_SENDING_ENABLED=true` and applicable tenant policy. Operator mail has a separate gate. Dunning dedupe uses `reb:past-due-email-sent:<tenant>:<pastDueSince>` and is released on suppressed/failed delivery; webhook success alone is not contact proof. Do not enable mail/env flags to fix a case without `[JACOB]` authorization.
3. Verify payer before giving an existing authorized portal/payment-update path. The operator portal uses the tenant's stored Stripe customer, not a general agency payer selector. Do not send a portal session to a requester solely because they are an agency member. `[JACOB]` approve contact and any live billing action; never ask for card details by email.
4. Inspect configured Stripe retries/dunning before proposing a manual retry; actual schedule is unknown until inspected. No local runbook promise of automatic retries or a specific recovery date. Coordinate provider action once and verify invoice state to avoid duplicate charging.
5. On `invoice.paid`, reconcile cleared `subscriptionPastDueSince` and status; a $0 `subscription_create` invoice retains trial treatment rather than proving paid recurring service. Verify account/business mirrors separately because synchronization is best effort.
6. If unrecovered, `[JACOB]` decide agreed follow-up/service treatment. [Subscription gate](../../src/lib/subscription.ts) has three-day past-due grace only where `requireActiveSubscription` is used and the Stripe price gate is configured; missing timestamps are lenient and grandfathered/comped exceptions exist. Do not describe this as an automatic site shutdown. [Public billing regression](../../src/__tests__/business-billing.test.ts) covers ungated public pages, v1, proxy, booking and lead paths. This task does not rerun that behavior test or prove production flags.

**After #283 / agency payer flow:** agency wholesale failure must not be treated as permission to take its client's public site offline. Offer direct-pay transition only through implemented accepted payer/provider flows and approved terms; never silently transfer debt or charge the business. Read the current per-party payer implementation when that stream lands; this baseline remains owner-based.

## Payer and payment-method changes

**Available today:** for the same authorized payer, use the existing configured Stripe portal to update its payment method where enabled; confirm provider outcome. Updating an email or local package amount is not replacing the payer's financial agreement.

Existing [payer-transition code](../../src/platform/work-economics/payer-transitions.ts) and [migration](../../supabase/migrations/20260918140000_workspace_payer_transitions.sql) cover a verified person accepting future job-economics payer responsibility. They do not move Stripe subscriptions or grant access to customer records. Do not claim an agency-to-business subscription switch is implemented.

For an actual billing-party change, prepare this plan and keep it pending until supported implementation and authority exist:

1. Verify current payer authority and proposed successor identity/party. Separate payer change from agency-of-record, service provider, data ownership and membership. Record old/new agreements, effective time, price, proration/cycle, outstanding balance, remaining obligations and no-downtime plan. `[JACOB]` approve commercial treatment; successor explicitly accepts before its first charge.
2. Inspect existing Stripe subscriptions and any active Checkout session. Do not generate a new subscription link merely to update email/card. Existing stored customer is reused; a new `customerEmail` input does not update its email. A parallel subscription can double-bill.
3. Select the supported future transaction path: new party/customer/subscription and coordinated cancellation/credit if required, exact effective dates and duplicate-charge guard. `[JACOB]` authorizes each live change. Preserve prior invoices, receipts, payer and accepted jobs; never relabel past charges to the successor. Do not cancel first and hope the successor pays.
4. Verify successor billing and correct local mapping/mirror, old subscription schedule, invoice/proration and unchanged public service. If one step lands and another fails, retain accepted financial IDs, open a reconciliation case and stop blind retry. Any compensating credit/refund follows its own approved operation.
5. **After #283 — agency/business payer, resale or connected account changes:** use the implemented party model and payer/provider transition stream; verify both Strelva wholesale and agency retail obligations. Account configurations, active capabilities, agreement/attribution boundaries and ledger reconciliation must all be ready. #283 does not itself implement subscription transfer, proration, split reversal or exit. End attribution/fees only through the accepted implemented path; preserve historic earnings and records.

## Webhook failures and safe replay

Inspect event ID/type/account/environment, signature/config error, current Stripe object, local handler outcome and idempotency state before deciding replay. Source: [webhook route](../../src/app/api/billing/webhook/route.ts); failure evidence includes [mode-guard tests](../../src/__tests__/billing-webhook-mode-guard.test.ts). No production replay is authorized here.

| Observed response/state | Interpretation and next action |
| --- | --- |
| 400 signature invalid / 500 missing billing config | Verify endpoint/environment/secret through authorized access; `[JACOB]` config repair, then provider delivery retry |
| 200 ignored test/live mismatch | Intentional guard, no billing mutation. Correct event/environment; do not bypass it |
| 200 duplicate | Recorded processed event, not proof every side effect/mirror/email succeeded. Inspect outcomes; resend usually cannot repair them |
| 503 current processing / idempotency unavailable | Processing lease or Redis authority issue. Recover store/inspect effects before retry; production missing/failing Redis fails closed |
| 500 handler failure / failed event | Inspect `stripe:event:error:<evt>` and accepted effects, prepare exact recovery; retry only after cause and duplication risk are assessed |
| 200 unknown tenant / unsupported event | Can be marked processed without intended local mutation. Repair mapping or missing implementation separately; blind replay will not fix it |

`stripe:event:<evt>` stores processing/processed/failed with a 30-day TTL, five-minute stale threshold and 60-second reclaim lock. Stale-claim retry is not proof that no prior effect occurred. Event-order key `stripe:tenant:last_event:<tenant>` expires after 30 days; strictly older timestamps skip the tenant status patch and its business mirror, but downstream allowance/account/notification paths can still execute. Equal timestamps can apply the patch. Ordering-store failure alerts but processing continues; processed-mark failure can be swallowed. Verify actual provider state and all local projections, not a lock key alone. Do not routinely delete idempotency/order keys or invent a new event ID to force delivery.

`[JACOB]` approve Stripe Dashboard resend only for the exact existing event after the handler/store issue is fixed and accepted effects assessed. A processed event with failed downstream accounting/mirror/mail needs targeted repair, not a second charge or blanket webhook replay. **After #283** use the separate implemented Connect endpoint/secret, `event.account`/v2 event context and connected-account mappings; do not send Connect events to this platform-subscription handler as a substitute.

## Incident escalation, closure and readiness

Duplicate payment, wrongful payer mapping, lost accepted financial evidence or broad billing failure enters the [incident procedure](./incident-and-status.md). Prepare a bounded stop; `[JACOB]` approves live changes. [Rollback](./rollback.md) changes code, not money; reconcile Stripe and tenant/account snapshots before reopening the affected gated action. No whole-database restore or local status edit can undo a financial effect.

Close a billing case only with requester/authority, approved operation, provider confirmation, local reconciliation status, communication outcome, outstanding deadline owner and labor/loss entries. Record gross amount, refunded/disputed amount, actual fees, recovered transfers, unrecovered loss and human minutes separately. They feed the [support cost model](./support-model.md#cost-per-agency-and-capacity); a refund is not simply negative support labor.
