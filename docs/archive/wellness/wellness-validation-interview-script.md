# Studio-Owner Validation Interviews — Script

**Goal:** find out if small studios will actually pay a premium for a better-designed, migration-safe site+booking, *before* we build the heavy stuff. 5-8 calls, 20-30 min each.

**Who to talk to:** 1-5 person studios, reformer Pilates focus. Get a mix on purpose:
- 2-3 like Cove (on Acuity or similar, budget/light) — Jazz can intro some.
- 2-3 on Mindbody/Walla/Momence (the paying segment — this is where the money is).
- Find them via Jazz's network, local studios, IG DMs, Pilates studio-owner Facebook groups.

---

## The 5 things we're trying to learn (kill or confirm)

1. **The hinge:** will they pay a *premium* for genuinely better design? (Everything rests on this.)
2. Is the split site-vs-booking a real felt pain, or do they not care?
3. Is *migration fear* the main thing keeping unhappy owners stuck?
4. Is class/reformer management actually painful on their current tool?
5. Price anchor: what they pay now, what they'd pay for the bundle.

---

## Ground rules (read before every call)

This is a **Mom Test** interview. The whole point is to get truth, not encouragement.

- **Do NOT pitch the idea.** The second they know what you're building, they start being nice. Talk about *their* world.
- **Ask about the past and specifics, not the future and hypotheticals.** "What did you do last time X happened" beats "would you use a tool that X." Hypothetical yeses are worthless.
- **Follow the money and the emotion.** When they get annoyed or mention paying for something, dig in.
- **Shut up and let them talk.** Silence pulls out the real answer.
- **Commitments are the only real validation.** Time, money, reputation, referrals. "Sounds great" is not data. "Can I get early access / here are 2 owners you should talk to" is.

## The conversation

### Warm-up
- "Tell me about your studio — how long, how many instructors, what you offer?"
- "How's business been lately?"

### How they run today (current behavior)
- "Walk me through how a client books a class right now — from opening their phone to showing up."
- "What are you using for that? And separately, what's your website on?"
- "Roughly what are you paying per month across all of it?"

### Pain (past behavior, not opinions)
- "When's the last time your booking tool or website actually annoyed you? What happened?"
- "How do you handle a class that fills up right now?" *(waitlist pain)*
- "What about no-shows and late cancels — how's that handled today?"
- "Have you ever looked at switching tools? What made you look? …What stopped you?" *(This one question tests migration fear AND switching triggers.)*

### Design / site (indirect — never ask "is design important")
- "How do you feel about your current website?"
- "Where do most new clients find you and decide to try you?"
- "Have you ever paid anyone to design or improve your site or brand?" *(past spend on design = the strongest possible signal for the hinge)*
- "Do clients ever say anything about your site or booking experience?"

### Class / reformer specifics
- "Do clients pick their specific reformer, or just a general spot? How does that work now?"
- "Anything about managing the class schedule that's clunky?"

### Migration (surface the fear)
- "If you ever did switch, what would you be most scared of losing or breaking?"
- "Have you ever moved client or payment data between tools? How'd that go?"

### Only near the end — willingness signals
Still keep it grounded, not a pitch:
- "If a tool fixed [the specific things they complained about], what would have to be true for you to actually switch?"
- "What would you expect something like that to cost?" *(let THEM say the number)*
- Then the real commitment asks:
  - "I'm building something in this space and want a few studios shaping it — would you want early access as a design partner?"
  - "Could you intro me to 1-2 other studio owners who'd have strong opinions on this?"

---

## What to listen for

**Green flags (real signal):**
- They've already *paid* to fix it (a designer, a second tool, a VA).
- They've actually tried to switch, or are mid-thinking about it.
- They light up about one specific, concrete frustration.
- They offer intros without being pushed, or ask "when can I get it?"
- They name a price unprompted.

**Red flags (polite lies):**
- "Yeah that sounds nice / that'd be great" with no story behind it.
- No past action — never tried to fix it, never looked at alternatives.
- Can't name a specific frustration when asked.
- Only gets excited *after* you describe your idea.
- Won't commit to anything (time, intro, early access).

---

## Field cheat sheet (keep this on the call)

1. Walk me through how a client books right now.
2. What are you on, and what do you pay total?
3. Last time it annoyed you — what happened?
4. Ever tried to switch? What stopped you?
5. Ever paid to improve your site/brand?
6. How do you handle a full class / reformer spots today?
7. If you switched, what would scare you most about it?
8. What would you expect a better all-in-one to cost?
9. Want early access? Know 2 others I should talk to?

---

## After each call — log in 2 lines
- Segment (Acuity-light / Mindbody-paying), current stack + spend.
- Did they show a **green flag** (paid to fix, tried to switch, offered intro, named a price)? Y/N — and the one sharpest quote.

**Decision rule:** if most of the paying-segment owners show real green flags on design + would switch, green-light Stage 2. If it's mostly polite "sounds nice" with no past action, this is a design-led services play, not a SaaS.
