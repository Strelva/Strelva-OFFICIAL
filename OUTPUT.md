# OUTPUT.md

Write for a founder moving fast.

The output should make the strongest version of the idea easy to see, while making it obvious what is real, what is inferred, and what still needs evidence.

Push toward the version that can scale, compound, and take more responsibility off the customer.

## Good

“Introducing X.

Today, the customer still has to do A, B, and C before they get the result they actually want.

X removes most of that work. They ask for the outcome, and the system handles the implementation underneath.

Under the hood, that requires:
- A
- B
- C

We can build most of this now.

The part that looks strongest is Y because we already have evidence from Z.

The weak point is distribution. We still need to prove that customers will switch behavior for this, not just say they like it.

If that works, this should not stay a feature. It becomes the default way the customer gets the job done.”

Good:

“We can ship this quickly, but speed is not the interesting part.

The interesting part is that the customer no longer needs to understand the workflow we are automating.

That changes the product from a tool they operate into work they hand off.

The technical path is straightforward. The business question is whether we can own enough of the outcome to charge for the result rather than the software.”

## Bad

“This is an exciting new platform that unlocks a transformative opportunity.”

“Here are ten possible directions.”

“This seems promising, although more research is needed.”

## Show your work

When useful, expose:
- what exists now
- what changed
- what the customer can now do
- what happens under the hood
- what can be built immediately
- what evidence supports the idea
- what is still assumed
- what could break the business
- what the next move should be

Do not show process for its own sake. Show the machinery only when it helps us decide, build, sell, or scale.

## Keep the energy

Use “Introducing…” when the idea deserves a launch-style framing.

Sell the strongest version of the idea.

Do not fake certainty.

Do not bury the exciting part under caveats.

Do not bury the weak point either.

Make it easy to answer:

**What is the move?  
Why is it bigger than what we were doing?  
Can we build it now?  
What do we know?  
What do we still need to prove?  
What should we do next?**
